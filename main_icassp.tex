\begin{abstract}
Cross-corpus failure in audio deepfake detection is often a failure of the
decision threshold rather than of score ordering: a released detector in our
In-the-Wild evaluation has ROC-AUC 0.9171 but 39.08\% accuracy at threshold
0.5. Confident-tail self-training, which pseudo-labels the lowest- and
highest-scoring target clips, exploits the ordering that survives. Over 79
adaptation runs spanning four released checkpoints, two corpora, spoof
prevalence 0.01 to 0.972 and budgets 0.02 to 0.50, it proves to be almost
purely an operating-point procedure: the accuracy its scores can attain shifts
by a median of 0.12 points while the gap between that ceiling and the accuracy
obtained at threshold 0.5 shifts by 14.87, and in no run does the ceiling move
further. Where the new operating point lands follows a counting quantity. A
bucket assigned class $c$ with realized fraction $b_c$ of a pool with prior
$\pi_c$ cannot be purer than $\min(1,\pi_c/b_c)$, so a fraction
$\Delta=\sum_c(b_c-\pi_c)^{+}$ of the pool is necessarily mislabeled. The
post-adaptation gap increases with $\Delta$ in 12 of the 14 strata that vary
the budget, and adaptation improves accuracy exactly when that gap ends below
the source's, on 79 of 79 runs. Purity orders none of this, and neither does
the budget: $q=0.50$ helps at 37\% spoof prevalence while $q=0.30$ destroys
accuracy at 97\%, and on a balanced pool a perfectly pure selection still costs
$9.6\pm0.8$ points over three seeds.
\end{abstract}
\begin{keywords}
audio deepfake detection, test-time adaptation, label shift, pseudo-labels
\end{keywords}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
Self-supervised speech representations paired with anti-spoofing back-ends
provide strong audio deepfake detectors~\cite{wav2vec2aug,aasist}, but transfer
across recording conditions and synthesis systems remains difficult~\cite{itw}.
A target-domain failure can lie in the score ordering, in the decision
threshold, or in both: a released detector in our In-the-Wild evaluation has
ROC-AUC 0.9171 but 39.08\% accuracy at threshold 0.5.

Test-time adaptation (TTA) updates a source model from unlabeled target
observations, and both entropy minimization~\cite{tent} and pseudo-label
self-training can reinforce incorrect predictions. We examine one self-training
rule, assigning the bottom and top score quantiles to bona fide and spoof, and
ask what it actually changes. It is not a rule proposed elsewhere: it is our
own prior design, an instance of confidence-based pseudo-label
selection~\cite{fixmatch,shot} with a fixed budget per tail. We find it changes
the operating point and little else, and that a counting constraint on the
budget predicts where that point lands.

Class imbalance in adaptation is established ground: Label Shift
Adapter~\cite{lsa} adapts parameters to estimated target proportions,
RLSbench~\cite{rlsbench} studies joint label and conditional shift, black-box
shift estimation (BBSE)~\cite{bbse} recovers target priors from a source
confusion matrix, and T$^2$A~\cite{t2a} treats deepfake TTA by
uncertainty-aware negative learning. Our claim is narrower and empirical: what
this rule changes, and the counting quantity that predicts it.

\section{Tail selection and adaptation}
\label{sec:method}
Let $y=1$ denote spoof, $s_\theta(x)\in[0,1]$ the detector's spoof score, and
the unlabeled adaptation pool have $N$ clips with unknown class fractions
$\pi_c$. Target labels are used only for evaluation, controlled resampling and
explicitly marked oracle analyses.

\subsection{A necessary condition for pure pseudo-labels}
A bucket $C_c$ assigned pseudo-label $c$, with realized fraction
$b_c=|C_c|/N>0$, contains at most $\pi_c N$ correct examples. Thus
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{purity}(C_c)\leq\min\{1,\pi_c/b_c\}.
 \label{eq:bound}
\end{equation}
This is a finite-pool counting bound and makes no assumption about the model.
An update, however, does not consume a purity: it consumes wrongly labeled
examples. The same counting fact says that the selection necessarily mislabels
a fraction
\begin{equation}
 \Delta=\sum_c(b_c-\pi_c)^{+}
 \label{eq:excess}
\end{equation}
of the pool. Purity is a within-bucket rate and $\Delta$ a share of the pool,
and the two order configurations differently: a small bucket can have very low
purity while mislabeling almost nothing. Section~\ref{sec:results} finds that
$\Delta$ orders the observed outcomes and that purity does not.

For exact symmetric tails, $b_0=b_1=q$, both statements reduce to
$\Delta=(q-\min\{\pi,1-\pi\})^{+}$, so perfect purity is possible only if
\begin{equation}
 q\leq\min\{\pi,1-\pi\},\qquad \pi=\pi_1.
 \label{eq:necessary}
\end{equation}
At 97.22\% spoof prevalence a bucket labeling 30\% of the pool bona fide has
purity at most 9.27\%, and no amount of AUC removes that. Feasibility is not
sufficiency: score overlap contaminates feasible tails, and learning tolerates
impure labels when there are few of them. That is why we test
\eqref{eq:excess} rather than \eqref{eq:necessary}.

A prior allocation uses nominal fractions $q_c=2q\hat\pi_c$, keeping the total
selection fraction $2q$. With exact buckets and a correct prior, $q\leq1/2$
ensures $q_c\leq\pi_c$; with estimated priors it instead requires
$\hat\pi_c/\pi_c\leq1/(2q)$, a limit on overestimation rather than a safety
theorem. A smaller symmetric $q$ needs no point estimate, but a guaranteed
feasible nonzero value still needs a lower bound on the minority prevalence.

\textbf{Quantile ties.} Our runs use inclusive thresholds, $s\leq Q(q_0)$ and
$s\geq Q(1-q_1)$, with spoof assignments taking precedence on overlap, so
realized sizes differ from nominal budgets. Equations~\eqref{eq:bound} and
\eqref{eq:excess} are evaluated at the realized $b_c$ throughout; we never read
a nominal budget as an exact sample count.

\subsection{Prior estimation and objective}
For $M_{ij}=P_{\mathcal S}(\hat y=j\mid y=i)$ and a target prediction histogram
$h$ from source predictions on the adaptation pool, BBSE solves
$M^\top\hat p=h$. We estimate $M$ from up to 4,000 class-balanced clips of the
checkpoint's training corpus, clip to $[10^{-4},1-10^{-4}]$ and renormalize;
using training rather than held-out data makes its error rates optimistic.

At each of four passes, the current model scores the adaptation pool and
refreshes pseudo-labels. For a mini-batch $B$ and its selected subset $B_C$,
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal L_B ={}&
 \frac{1}{|B_C|}\sum_{x\in B_C}\!\mathrm{CE}(p_\theta(x),\tilde y_x)
 \\ &+\frac{\lambda}{2|B|}\sum_{x\in B}
 \|p_\theta(a(x))-\mathrm{sg}[p_\theta(x)]\|_2^2,
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
where $p_\theta=(1-s_\theta,s_\theta)$, the CE term is omitted when $B_C$ is
empty, and $\lambda=0.3$. The consistency term uses all batch examples and both
probability components. The perturbation is per-clip gain sampled uniformly
from $[0.7,1.3]$ plus Gaussian noise with standard deviation 0.005. Only the
last four transformer blocks' LayerNorm affine parameters and the classifier
head are updated; BatchNorm statistics stay frozen. Adam uses learning rate
$10^{-4}$ and batch size 32. The released DeepFense models have 16,706 trainable
parameters under this rule. A fixed-confidence control instead selects scores
at most 0.05 or at least 0.95, with the same objective.

\section{Evaluation protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}
\textbf{Checkpoints and pools.} We use DeepFense XLS-R 300M + AASIST
checkpoints trained on ASVspoof2019-LA with seeds 2, 42 and
240~\cite{deepfense,deepfenseweights}, denoted DF-2, DF-42, DF-240, and a
WaveFake-trained SSL-AASIST checkpoint WF~\cite{shiftyspeech}. Audio is
16\,kHz, cropped to 64,000 samples for DeepFense and 64,600 for WF.

The official ASVspoof2021-DF metadata has 533,928 eval trials among 611,829
across three phases~\cite{asvspoof21}. Our available \emph{eval-only} subset
holds 400,435 of them, of which 389,299 are spoof and 11,136 bona fide, so
97.219\% spoof, covering 75.00\% of the spoof class and 74.89\% of bona fide.
The missing shard rules out a full-set comparison; all comparisons use the
same trials.

The main DF run adapts on 20,000 clips with adaptation seed 0; we report the
available pool and its 380,435-clip disjoint remainder. Seeds 1 and 2 adapt
20,000 clips of a fixed 50,000-trial subset (seed 12345), sensitivity checks on
overlapping data rather than nine independent datasets. In-the-Wild uses all
31,779 clips (11,816 spoof, 37.182\%) with 20,000 adaptation clips.

\textbf{Prevalence intervention.} We resample either corpus to a target spoof
proportion (seed 4242) by thinning whichever class must shrink: In-the-Wild to
eight priors from 0.01 to 0.97 including its native 0.372, and DF to 0.01,
0.10, 0.50 and 0.90, the last a balanced pool. Every resampled pool adapts on
8,000 clips, so the optimization budget is identical at every prevalence and a
disjoint remainder always remains; the evaluation pool size still varies with
prevalence, which the experiment does not separate from it.

\textbf{Metrics and controls.} We recompute metrics from saved float32
probabilities. ROC-AUC assigns half credit to ties; EER linearly interpolates
the crossing of FPR and FNR on the ROC, grouping identical scores. Interpolated
EER need not correspond to an attainable deterministic threshold. We also
report accuracy, balanced accuracy, and the oracle threshold gap
$G_\tau=\max_t\operatorname{Acc}(s,t)-\operatorname{Acc}(s,\tau)$, whose
maximum ranges over all distinct thresholds including constant predictions. It
uses evaluation labels and is a diagnostic of threshold headroom, not a
deployable method or a calibration error.

Two label-free controls keep source scores fixed and pick a threshold from
adaptation scores: their median, or their $(1-\hat\pi)$-quantile using BBSE.
Both preserve AUC and EER; neither is a claim of Bayes optimality.

We additionally report fixed-configuration entropy adaptation (Tent-style)
under the same update settings~\cite{tent}.\footnote{Its scores saturate:
98.07\% lie within $10^{-6}$ of 1.0 but only 93.02\% exactly at 1.0, and
conflating the two makes its AUC look impossible. With exact ties grouped it
reaches 4.28\% EER and 0.9765 AUC against the source's 4.51\% and 0.9923, a
gain near one operating point coexisting with worse integrated ranking. The
scoring convention matters too: ROC interpolation gives the ETA ablation
12.31\% EER where a nearest-point rule gives 7.44\%. Three further published
TTA methods were run the same way, as controlled variants rather than tuned
reproductions: an ETA reliability-only ablation without redundancy or Fisher
terms (EER 12.31\%, and an all-spoof decision at threshold 0.5 on DF-42 and
DF-240), SAR with recovery threshold scaled to $(0.2/\ln1000)\ln2$ (4.48\%),
and IM-PL, a SHOT-inspired information-maximization control with
probability-space centroid pseudo-labels that freezes the head and updates
16,384 parameters (6.35\%)~\cite{eata,sar,shot}. None is a tuned reproduction
and none establishes anything about those methods' published performance;
their audited rows are in the released tables.}

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
\subsection{Adaptation moves the operating point, not the ranking}
Across all 79 symmetric-budget runs -- four checkpoints, two corpora, spoof
prevalence 0.01 to 0.972, budgets $q$ from 0.02 to 0.50 and three adaptation
seeds on eight cells -- adaptation leaves almost untouched the accuracy its
scores can attain, and moves only where the decision sits. The best accuracy
attainable over all thresholds shifts by a median of 0.12 points; the gap
between that ceiling and the accuracy obtained at threshold 0.5 shifts by 14.87
points, and in no run does the ceiling move further than the gap
(Fig.~\ref{fig:prev}(a)). ROC-AUC shifts by a median of 0.12 points. This is why the metrics disagree so violently: the same DF-2 run
that changes accuracy by 35.9 points changes EER by 1.33.

\subsection{Contamination sets where the operating point lands}
The quantity that predicts the new operating point is $\Delta$. In 12 of the 14
checkpoint/prevalence strata that vary the budget, the post-adaptation
threshold gap increases with $\Delta$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:prev}(b)), and in the
seven whose source threshold is already well placed -- a source gap under 17
points -- it does so strictly, six of them at $\rho=+1$. The two exceptions
both have badly placed sources, DF-42 at $\pi=0.01$ leaving 95.1 points on the
table: there the smallest budgets cannot move the model at all and larger ones
close the gap rather than open it.

With the invariance above, this says exactly when the rule is worth applying:
adaptation raises accuracy if and only if its post-adaptation gap ends below
the source's, which holds on 79 of 79 runs. Neither purity nor the budget
orders the same outcomes. At the native In-the-Wild prior $q=0.50$ -- the
largest symmetric budget there is, labeling the entire pool -- leaves a
7.43-point gap and gains 7.47 points of balanced accuracy, while $q=0.15$ at
$\pi=0.01$ leaves 28.17 and gains 2.64; a tail of purity 0.107 gains 9.36
points where one of purity 0.320 loses 0.37. Nor is a clean selection
sufficient: on a balanced DF pool, where $\Delta=0$ and the measured purity is
1.000, adaptation still moves the gap from 7.38 to $17.4\pm0.9$ and costs
$9.6\pm0.8$ points of accuracy across three seeds.

\subsection{Anchoring on ASVspoof2021-DF}
On DF-2 (Table~\ref{tab:df}) symmetric $q=0.3$ raises EER from 4.51\% to
5.84\%, lowers balanced accuracy from 88.28\% to 80.89\% and accuracy from
98.87\% to 62.96\%, against a 97.22\% always-spoof baseline and a 35.38-point
threshold gap. Allocating the tails by a BBSE prior estimate instead reaches
4.06\% EER and 98.95\% accuracy, but 82.34\% balanced accuracy, below the
source: one effective remedy, not a uniformly best one, and not an improvement
for both classes.

The same pattern holds on DF-42 and DF-240. Across the nine checkpoint/run
combinations BBSE has lower EER than the symmetric budget in 9/9, and than the
source in 8/9 on the available pool and 7/9 under disjoint evaluation. A paired
stratified bootstrap over trials (2,000 replicates, resampled within class so
the prior stays fixed) separates the two: all nine symmetric differences have
95\% intervals excluding zero, but only 4/9 and 5/9 of the source differences
do, and DF-240's is $-0.02$ points $[-0.14,+0.12]$. These counts are
descriptive, not independent evidence.

Re-thresholding alone reproduces that accuracy: a frozen-source BBSE threshold
gives 98.88\% against the source's 98.87\%, while the median gives 59.06\%.

\subsection{Corpus shift and prior estimation}
At the native In-the-Wild prior the budget has no counting obstruction and
improves accuracy on three of four checkpoints. For DF-42 it raises AUC from
0.9171 to 0.9473 and accuracy from 39.08\% to 82.39\%, though a frozen-source
median threshold already reaches 78.10\%. BBSE estimates 97.9\% spoof on this
37.2\%-spoof corpus and lands at 14.54\% EER and 47.99\% accuracy, against
11.59\%/91.28\% for a non-deployable oracle. BBSE is identifiable under label
shift with an invertible confusion matrix~\cite{bbse}, but under unrestricted
conditional shift $P_{\mathcal T}(\hat y=1)=\pi\mathrm{TPR}_{\mathcal T}+
(1-\pi)\mathrm{FPR}_{\mathcal T}$ cannot determine all three unknowns, which is
why a source confusion matrix may fail to transfer.

\textbf{The deployment direction.} Real pools are mostly bona fide, so we
extend the sweep to $\pi\in\{0.01,0.02,0.05,0.10\}$, where the spoof tail is the
constrained one. The mirror image appears: at $\pi=0.01$, $q=0.3$ takes
accuracy from 65.72\% to 51.90\% and balanced accuracy from 82.69\% to
75.71\%, with the damage carried entirely by false alarms (FPR 48.58\% against
34.62\%, FNR 0.00\% in both). A small budget does not break where
\eqref{eq:necessary} says it must: its spoof tail is bounded at 52.7\% purity
and measured at 33.3\%, yet reaches 97.00\% accuracy. Every arm at every
prevalence has EER between 4.95\% and 5.27\% while accuracy spans 51.90\% to
97.00\%.

\textbf{Budget sweeps.} Sweeping $q$ at fixed prevalence traces the relation
directly, and repeating three points of each sweep over three adaptation seeds
shows the ordering is not seed noise. At $\pi=0.01$ balanced accuracy falls
from $+12.00\pm0.17$ at $\Delta=0.049$ through $+3.35\pm0.62$ to
$-5.19\pm1.63$ at $0.289$; at $\pi=0.97$ it falls from $+11.62\pm0.10$ through
$+5.43\pm0.46$ to $-1.80\pm0.20$. Each step between budgets is five to sixteen
times the seed spread, and both orderings survive disjoint evaluation.

\textbf{Prior estimation in that direction.} BBSE, which repaired the
high-prevalence collapse, does not transfer: it estimates 35.9--41.8\% spoof on
pools that are 1.1--9.7\% spoof and changes accuracy by $-1.0$ to $+4.7$
points, where a prior-free $q=0.02$ gains 27.1 to 31.3.

\section{Discussion and limitations}
On these detectors, confident-tail adaptation chooses an operating point rather
than improving a detector, which is a narrower reading than the rule is usually
given and an easier one to deploy responsibly: use it where a source threshold
is believed to be badly placed, and bound $\Delta$ otherwise. The counting
obstruction is what makes $\Delta$ computable, and it is useful only in that
currency: a purity ceiling orders nothing, while the share of the pool that
must be mislabeled orders the resulting operating point wherever the source
threshold was worth preserving. It bounds the damage and does not promise a
benefit; a balanced pool with a perfectly pure selection still lost 8.6 points.

The evidence is four released checkpoints from one architecture family, two
corpora, an incomplete DF eval partition, one adaptation recipe, and three
adaptation seeds on eight of the cells, on pools that share audio within a
corpus. $\Delta$
uses realized bucket sizes and the true prior, so it is a diagnostic, not a
label-free rule; making it one needs a prior estimate, and ours fail exactly
where the deployment cases lie.
