\title{Ranking Transfers, Thresholds Don't:\\
Unsupervised Test-Time Adaptation for Cross-Corpus Audio Deepfake Detection}

\begin{abstract}
Audio deepfake detectors generalise poorly across corpora despite near-perfect
in-domain accuracy. We show that a fine-tuned self-supervised detector's
\emph{ranking} (ROC-AUC) largely transfers cross-corpus even when its decision
\emph{threshold} does not calibrate, and put that asymmetry to work: where
ranking survives, the model's most-confident predictions on unlabeled target
audio are reliable enough to self-train on. We exploit this with a
stable unsupervised \emph{test-time adaptation} (TTA) method: self-train on
confident-ranked pseudo-labels and enforce channel-consistency, adapting only
LayerNorm and the classifier head, with no target labels and no source data
at test time. On a leave-one-corpus-out protocol over four targets
(ASVspoof2019, a LibriSpeech-based TTS corpus, In-the-Wild, and an Arabic
set), ten seeds each, EER improves on three (ASVspoof2019 $5.03\!\to\!3.47\%$,
In-the-Wild $12.78\!\to\!11.33\%$, Arabic $22.50\!\to\!20.99\%$; two survive
Holm correction across four per-target Wilcoxon tests) and is neutral on the
fourth, which is also the target with the weakest source ranking (AUC $0.73$).
The method is not tied to our own detector: applied unmodified to four
\emph{publicly released} checkpoints we did not train, it improves In-the-Wild
EER on all four ($16.41\!\to\!10.10\%$ on the weakest, $5.04\!\to\!4.08\%$ on
the strongest) by adapting $16{,}706$ parameters ($0.005\%$).
Entropy-minimisation TTA (Tent) is instead unpredictably catastrophic (four of
ten ASVspoof2019 seeds at or near chance, with a standard deviation as large as
its mean), and a non-SSL backbone never reaches a ranking worth adapting. We
beat a domain-adversarial baseline (DANN), which additionally sees unlabeled
target audio during training, on two of four targets, and an
aggregation-separation baseline (ASDG-style) on three of four.
A proxy $\mathcal{A}$-distance analysis shows domain separability
does \emph{not} decrease after adaptation, ruling out domain-invariance as the
mechanism and supporting our ranking-calibration account instead.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
audio deepfake detection, anti-spoofing, test-time adaptation, cross-corpus

\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
Self-supervised (SSL) front-ends such as wav2vec\,2.0 and XLS-R, paired with
lightweight back-ends, reach very low in-domain error on standard anti-spoofing
benchmarks~\cite{wav2vec2aug,xlsrsls,aasist}. Deployed on audio from a
different source---new microphones, codecs, languages, or generators---accuracy
drops steeply, a well-documented generalisation gap~\cite{asdg,crossdomain,itw}.

We start from a diagnostic measurement: a fine-tuned XLS-R detector evaluated on
an unseen corpus keeps a high \textbf{ROC-AUC while its accuracy at a fixed
threshold collapses}---it still \emph{ranks} clips correctly but its operating
point is mis-calibrated. Because ranking transfers, its most-confident
predictions on unlabeled target audio are trustworthy enough to adapt on,
\emph{at test time}, without any labels.

Our contributions: (i) an operational use of the ranking--calibration
asymmetry---ranking transfers cross-corpus even when the threshold does
not---as the precondition that makes unsupervised self-training viable;
(ii) a simple, stable TTA method---confident-ranked
pseudo-label self-training plus channel-consistency, adapting only LayerNorm
and the head; (iii) a four-target, multilingual, ten-seed leave-one-corpus-out
evaluation against seven comparison points (source-only, Tent, BatchNorm-only
recalibration, self-training-only, a non-SSL backbone, DANN, and an
ASDG-style aggregation-separation baseline), including the
one target where the method does not help; (iv) a mechanistic analysis---proxy
$\mathcal{A}$-distance before/after adaptation---showing the gain does
\emph{not} come from erasing domain identity; and (v) evidence that the method
acts as a \emph{label-free wrapper for third-party detectors}, improving four
publicly released checkpoints spanning two independent training corpora without
retraining them.

\textbf{Related work.} Frozen or fine-tuned SSL front-ends with AASIST or
attentive back-ends dominate recent benchmarks~\cite{wav2vec2aug,xlsrsls,aasist,rawnet2};
we additionally test whether the cross-corpus gap is an SSL question at all via
a from-scratch, non-SSL backbone. Train-time domain generalisation (DG) methods align real and
separate fake across source domains (aggregation-separation~\cite{asdg}) or run
gradient-reversal domain-adversarial training over source and target jointly
(DANN)~\cite{dann}; both act at training time rather than at deployment.
Entropy minimisation (Tent)~\cite{tent} is the standard TTA recipe in vision
but, as we show, is unstable here; batch-statistics
recalibration~\cite{adabn} is a no-op for LayerNorm architectures. We borrow
the proxy $\mathcal{A}$-distance from Ben-David et al.~\cite{bendavid} to test
mechanism.

\section{Method}
Let $x$ be a waveform with label $y\in\{0,1\}$ (fake$=$1). A detector $f_\theta$
pools frame features into an embedding $e$ and outputs
$s_\theta(x)=\sigma(w^\top e+b)=P_\theta(y{=}1\mid x)$: XLS-R-300M with only its
top four transformer layers trainable, attentive temporal pooling, and a linear
head. Its normalisation is \emph{LayerNorm}, which carries no running batch
statistics, so a batch-statistics-recalibration baseline is architecturally a
no-op here and confidence-driven gradient adaptation is the minimal mechanism
that can move these layers at all.

\textbf{The ranking--calibration gap.} Ranking quality
$\mathrm{AUC}=\Pr[s_\theta(x^+){>}s_\theta(x^-)]$ is threshold-free and invariant
to reparametrisation of $s_\theta$; accuracy at a fixed threshold $\tau$ is not.
Empirically, AUC degrades far less than accuracy at $\tau{=}0.5$ under
source$\to$target shift (Fig.~\ref{fig:scores}). We therefore trust score
\emph{order}: the extreme quantiles of unlabeled target scores are
higher-precision pseudo-labels than a fixed threshold, even when $s_\theta$ is
mis-calibrated. The fixed-quantile split $Q_q$ vs.\ $Q_{1-q}$ implicitly
assumes both a reliable source ranking and roughly balanced target classes; we
scope the method to that regime, and Sec.~\ref{sec:results} and
Sec.~\ref{sec:lim} show what happens when each assumption breaks.

\textbf{Test-time adaptation.} Each epoch, we assign confident pseudo-labels
from the $q$- and $(1{-}q)$-quantiles of target scores, ignoring the middle;
self-train with cross-entropy on this confident set ($\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ST}}$);
and enforce prediction invariance under a stochastic channel perturbation
$a(\cdot)$ (gain$+$noise) via an MSE consistency term $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cons}}$
between clean and perturbed views (stop-gradient on the clean target). The
objective is
\begin{equation}
\min_{\{\gamma,\beta\},\,\phi}\ \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ST}}+\lambda\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cons}},
\qquad \lambda{=}0.3,\ q{=}0.3,\ E{=}4,
\end{equation}
optimising \emph{only} the LayerNorm affine parameters $\{\gamma,\beta\}$ of the
fine-tuned layers and the head $\phi$; the rest stays frozen. Restricting the
trainable set and using only confident tails prevents the degenerate collapse
to which entropy minimisation is prone.

\textbf{Domain divergence.} To test whether adaptation works by making source
and target indistinguishable in embedding space (as adversarial DG methods
explicitly optimise for), we compute a proxy $\mathcal{A}$-distance
$\hat d_{\mathcal{A}}=2(1{-}2\epsilon_h)$ following Ben-David et
al.~\cite{bendavid}, where $\epsilon_h$ is a linear domain classifier's
held-out error on frozen embeddings, before and after adaptation.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\textbf{Protocol.} Leave-one-corpus-out over four EER-capable targets:
ASVspoof2019-LA~\cite{asvspoof19}; D2, a corpus pairing LibriSpeech bona fide
speech with fakes from eight open TTS/voice-cloning systems (NaturalSpeech3,
FlashSpeech, VALL-E, XTTS, PromptTTS2, VoiceBox, SeedTTS and a commercial API);
In-the-Wild~\cite{itw}; and the Arabic Audio Deepfake set
(ArAD)~\cite{arad}. For each held-out target,
the source pool merges the other three corpora with a 38-language MLAAD
subset~\cite{mlaad} (fake-only) for generator/language diversity; held-out target audio,
including its MLAAD-overlapping language content, is never in any source pool,
and six MLAAD languages are held out of every source pool entirely to test
genuine unseen-language generalisation.

\textbf{Comparison points.} Source-only (no adaptation); Tent~\cite{tent}
(entropy minimisation, same trainable set as ours); BN-only (AdaBN-style
recalibration~\cite{adabn}, no gradient step); self-training-only (ours with
$\lambda{=}0$); Oracle (fine-tuned on target labels, a supervised upper bound);
RawNet2Lite (a from-scratch, non-SSL backbone in the RawNet2
family~\cite{rawnet2}, trained identically, to isolate whether robustness comes
from the SSL front-end); DANN~\cite{dann}, trained to convergence with the
held-out target's \emph{unlabeled} inputs drawn every step through the
gradient-reversal branch---strictly more privileged access than our method
has---and an ASDG-style aggregation-separation baseline~\cite{asdg}, which
aligns across the \emph{source} domains only and never sees target audio.

\textbf{Implementation.} 16\,kHz audio, 3\,s crops, top-4-layer XLS-R
fine-tuning, Adam lr\,$2{\times}10^{-4}$, batch 32, source trained 8 epochs.
Adaptation reuses the same batch size and crop length with
lr\,$1{\times}10^{-4}$ for $E{=}4$ passes; the perturbation $a(\cdot)$ is a
per-clip gain $g\!\sim\!\mathcal{U}(0.7,1.3)$ plus additive Gaussian noise
($\sigma{=}0.005$), and the head is carried over from the source model rather
than re-initialised. Every number in Tables~\ref{tab:dann} and \ref{tab:main},
and every significance test, is over ten seeds per target for ours and for each
baseline in those tables. The third-party-checkpoint, proxy
$\mathcal{A}$-distance and ASVspoof2021-DF results are smaller studies and we
give their run counts where they appear. Code and manifests:
\texttt{[URL withheld for review]}.

Table~\ref{tab:main} is the main result. Our method wins on three of four
targets: ASVspoof2019 ($5.03\!\to\!3.47\%$, $10/10$ seeds), Arabic
($22.50\!\to\!20.99\%$, $10/10$), In-the-Wild ($12.78\!\to\!11.33\%$, $8/10$);
on LibriSpeech-TTS it is EER-neutral ($33.54\!\to\!33.74\%$, improved in only
$5/10$ seeds), and that is also the target with the lowest source AUC ($0.73$
vs.\ $\geq\!0.84$ elsewhere). The ordering is consistent with our
ranking-precondition account, but the association is \emph{between} corpora,
not within them: the four target means give $r{=}{+}0.86$ ($n{=}4$,
$p{=}0.14$), while pooling all 40 (target, seed) points gives $r{=}{+}0.47$
($p{=}0.002$)---an ecological artefact of four clusters, since within each
target the sign is reversed or absent ($r{=}{-}0.77$, ${-}0.64$, ${-}0.64$,
${+}0.12$; Fig.~\ref{fig:aucgain}). We therefore report source AUC as a
four-corpus descriptive ordering, not a validated deployment rule.

\textbf{Significance.} Ten seeds per target make a \emph{per-target} Wilcoxon
test possible at all ($n{=}5$ has a combinatorial floor of $0.0625$; $n{=}10$
reaches $0.002$). Against source-only, ours is significant on ASVspoof2019 and
Arabic ($p{=}0.0020$ each) and In-the-Wild ($p{=}0.0293$), not on
LibriSpeech-TTS ($p{=}0.23$). Holm correction across the four keeps
ASVspoof2019 and Arabic ($p_{\mathrm{adj}}{=}0.0078$) but not In-the-Wild
($0.0586$), which we therefore call nominal. A 10k-resample bootstrap on the
ten \emph{per-seed EER differences} agrees, excluding zero on ASVspoof2019
$[{+}1.12,{+}2.02]$, In-the-Wild $[{+}0.43,{+}2.33]$ and Arabic
$[{+}1.10,{+}1.88]$, and including it on LibriSpeech-TTS $[{-}0.49,{+}0.09]$.
Pooled over all 40 pairs the method beats source-only ($p{=}2.4\times10^{-5}$),
Tent ($1.5\times10^{-7}$), self-training-only ($0.0056$) and both DG baselines
($p{<}10^{-4}$, 30/40 each); these pooled figures treat clustered pairs as
independent and are descriptive only.

\textbf{Tent is not merely unstable, it is unpredictably catastrophic.} Its
ASVspoof2019 per-seed EER is $4.16$, $3.91$, $3.04$, $49.52$, $3.42$, $49.44$,
$49.69$, $42.11$, $25.31$, $4.12\%$---five seeds close to source-only, four at
or near chance and one midway, with no warning sign; the standard deviation
($21.96$) is comparable to the mean ($23.47$). On Arabic it is a
\emph{consistent} failure instead ($47.35{\pm}5.03\%$, chance-level in $9/10$
seeds). Our method
shows no comparable collapse on any target (largest std $2.01$). The failure is
not specific to our source model, and
is a ranking failure first: run unmodified on two of the four public
checkpoints below (the strongest and the weakest), Tent collapses to chance on one
($16.41\!\to\!49.80\%$ EER, AUC $.918\!\to\!.502$)~\cite{deepfensehf} and leaves
EER near-unchanged on the other ($5.04\!\to\!4.52\%$) while still degrading AUC
($.986\!\to\!.979$)~\cite{shiftyspeech}---an EER collapse appears only where the
model had AUC headroom to lose.

\textbf{Transfer to third-party detectors.} Every result above adapts our own
source model, so we apply the published configuration unmodified to four
released detectors we did not train---one WaveFake-trained~\cite{shiftyspeech},
three ASVspoof2019-trained~\cite{deepfensehf} XLS-R+AASIST checkpoints, none
having seen In-the-Wild. EER and AUC improve on all four---$5.04\!\to\!4.08$,
$9.59\!\to\!9.06$, $8.41\!\to\!7.88$ and $16.41\!\to\!10.10\%$ EER, with AUC
$.986\!\to\!.990$, $.966\!\to\!.970$, $.970\!\to\!.973$ and
$.918\!\to\!.961$, over all $31{,}779$ clips---while adapting $16{,}706$
parameters ($0.005\%$).
Three of the four gains are modest ($0.52$--$0.96$ EER); the large one ($6.31$)
belongs to the checkpoint arriving worst-calibrated (accuracy $39.0\%$ at
threshold $0.5$ despite AUC $.918$)---the regime the precondition predicts. It
is not memorisation of the adaptation pool: adapting on half of In-the-Wild and
evaluating on the disjoint half gives $4.08\%$ vs.\ $4.06\%$ transductive, and
$10.21\%$ vs.\ $10.10\%$. The three ASVspoof2019 checkpoints, identical but for
training seed, span $8.41$--$16.41\%$ source EER---single-checkpoint
cross-corpus figures, ours included, carry a large and usually unreported error
bar.

\textbf{Backbone and prior DG baselines.} RawNet2Lite (no SSL pretraining)
stays near chance on every target (AUC $0.40$--$0.65$), confirming
cross-corpus viability starts from SSL pretraining, not the TTA step; retraining
it for 40 epochs with stronger regularisation does not close the gap, ruling
out undertraining as the explanation. Both train-time baselines are beaten
pooled over all 40 (target, seed) pairs ($30/40$ each), but at ten seeds the
per-target picture is narrower than a three-seed comparison suggested, and
differs between them. Against DANN---which additionally samples the held-out
target's unlabeled audio at every training step---we win on ASVspoof2019
($9/10$, $p{=}0.0098$) and Arabic ($10/10$, $p{=}0.0020$) and tie on
In-the-Wild ($5/10$) and LibriSpeech-TTS ($6/10$). Against ASDG---which trains
on source domains only---we win on ASVspoof2019 ($8/10$, $p{=}0.0098$),
In-the-Wild ($10/10$, $p{=}0.0020$) and Arabic ($10/10$, $p{=}0.0020$), with
ASDG better on LibriSpeech-TTS ($32.64\%$ vs.\ our $33.74\%$, $2/10$,
$p{=}0.16$). DANN is markedly worse on Arabic, about $3\times$ our adaptation
cost ($20.1$ vs.\ $6.9$ min per target-seed over ten seeds), and the less
seed-stable of the two (ASVspoof2019 EER ranges $3.23$--$14.85\%$); Tent
remains the least stable method overall by a wide margin. ASDG beats DANN on
three of four targets but plain source-only on two (Table~\ref{tab:dann}).
Neither is a reliable route to cross-corpus robustness here.

\textbf{Mechanism.} An inductive check (adapt on one half of the target,
evaluate on the disjoint half) confirms the gain is not transductive
memorisation. On one seed per target, the proxy $\mathcal{A}$-distance and a
linear domain-origin probe both increase slightly after adaptation rather than
decreasing (e.g.\ Arabic $\hat d_{\mathcal{A}}\!:1.545\!\to\!1.606$)---small
shifts, but in the opposite direction to what domain-invariance predicts.
Adaptation re-shapes the decision boundary using rank-reliable pseudo-labels
rather than erasing domain identity, consistent with DANN underperforming
despite explicitly optimising that quantity. A 38-language MLAAD breakdown
shows fake-clip recall is uniform across seen and unseen languages ($93.9\%$
vs.\ $93.3\%$).

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:lim}
The method is transductive (unlabeled target inputs at adaptation time, the
standard TTA setting); an inductive check reproduces every result including the
LibriSpeech-TTS null. We report per-target, per-seed numbers throughout, so the
limitations below can be read against the evidence:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Needs an already-reliable source ranking---and we cannot say how
much of that is SSL.} A from-scratch or low-AUC starting point is not a regime
we can rescue (Fig.~\ref{fig:aucgain}). Our non-SSL backbone never reaches a
ranking worth adapting (AUC $0.40$--$0.65$), so it fails the precondition
before TTA becomes applicable and we did not run adaptation on it; all four
third-party checkpoints the wrapper improves are themselves SSL front-ends.
\item \textbf{Class balance is a second precondition, alongside AUC.} On the
official ASVspoof2021-DF protocol (train on 2019-LA alone; $97.2\%$-spoof eval
pool, of which we score $75\%$), source-only reaches $26.33\%$ EER and TTA
actively \emph{harms} ($42.46\%$). The cause is structural: the fixed-quantile
rule labels the bottom $q$ of the pool ``confident real'' whatever the true
prior, so under severe skew that bucket fills with mislabelled spoof and
self-training teaches the model that much of the fake class is real. Two
label-free corrections fail---a prevalence-weighted budget helps under one
source recipe ($32.58\%$) but not another ($42.46\%$), and a curriculum over
$q$ changes nothing ($42.50\%$)---both still worse than not adapting.
\item \textbf{Not a state-of-the-art detector.} Our source model is an XLS-R
encoder with a light attentive head trained on a leave-one-corpus-out pool, not
a system tuned for any single benchmark: on 2021-DF, published SSL-plus-AASIST
systems reach $8$--$9\%$ EER and specialised AASIST-class systems $2$--$3\%$,
against our $26.33\%$ source-only. The claim here is the \emph{relative} gain
from adaptation on a fixed backbone under an identical protocol, not a
competitive absolute result; the mechanism would have to be re-verified on a
stronger source model.
\item \textbf{Fixed hyperparameters.} $q,\lambda,E$ are chosen without target
labels and are not per-target optimal; a label-free schedule for $\lambda$ is
predictable from tail separation but pays nothing at our scale (fixed $\lambda$
is within $0.24$ EER of a per-point oracle), so $\lambda$ stays constant.
\item \textbf{Scale.} Four targets, ten seeds. Going from three to ten seeds
made the per-target tests possible and confirmed Tent's collapse as routine,
but shrank our DG margin to two per-target wins against DANN and three against
ASDG. Seeds do not buy corpora: the AUC ordering rests on four points
($n{=}4$, $p{=}0.14$), so the precondition is a hypothesis our data are
consistent with, not one they test.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
Cross-corpus audio deepfake detection fails not because ranking is lost but
because the operating point is mis-calibrated. A simple unsupervised test-time
adaptation exploiting this improves generalisation on three of four
leave-one-corpus-out targets---two surviving Holm correction---and is neutral
on the fourth, the one with the weakest source ranking. Whether source AUC
predicts the gain is suggested by our four corpora but not established by them
($r{=}{+}0.86$, $n{=}4$, $p{=}0.14$); settling it needs more corpora, not more
seeds. Entropy-minimisation TTA is unpredictably catastrophic, a non-SSL
backbone never reaches an adaptable ranking, and two train-time DG baselines
are beaten pooled over all 40 pairs---one despite seeing unlabeled target audio
during training, at $3\times$ our cost. A proxy divergence analysis puts the
mechanism at re-calibration, not domain-invariance; needing neither labels nor
retraining, the method also improves four released SSL detectors---a wrapper,
not a system.
