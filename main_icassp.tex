\title{Ranking Transfers, Thresholds Don't:\\
Prior-Corrected Test-Time Adaptation for Audio Deepfake Detection}

\begin{abstract}
Audio deepfake detectors generalise poorly across corpora despite near-perfect
in-domain accuracy. We show the failure is one of \emph{operating point}: a
fine-tuned self-supervised detector's \emph{ranking} (ROC-AUC) largely transfers
cross-corpus while its decision \emph{threshold} does not, so unlabeled target
audio can repair the operating point at test time. We then show that
confident-quantile test-time adaptation (TTA) can be sound only while its tail
budget satisfies $q\le\min(\pi,1{-}\pi)$: a counting bound caps pseudo-label
purity at $\min(1,\pi_{\mathrm{real}}/q)$ \emph{for any detector}. That holds
where TTA is measured and essentially nowhere else --- ASVspoof2021-DF is
$97.2\%$ spoof, In-the-Wild $37.2\%$ --- so at the published $q{=}0.3$ no model
can exceed $9.3\%$ purity on DF. Empirically, on the official DF eval
($400{,}435$ trials) that configuration destroys the operating point of three
released state-of-the-art checkpoints, driving accuracy from $98.8$--$99.1\%$ to
$62$--$65\%$; on the one where we also ran the published TTA baselines, three of
four degrade it as well, each through its own version of the assumption. Correcting the budget with a
label-free prior estimate ($\hat\pi{=}0.9715$ vs.\ a true $0.9708$) restores all
three and beats the symmetric budget on every one; both EER and AUC move, so no
re-thresholding rule accounts for it. The correction's boundary we measure
rather than assume: the prior is not identifiable from a model's own outputs, so
its estimate degrades exactly as the operating point does ($r{=}0.998$), and a
wrong prior is worse than none. Since $q$ is ours to choose and $\pi$ is not,
shrinking $q$ satisfies the condition without estimating anything.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
audio deepfake detection, anti-spoofing, test-time adaptation, label shift,
cross-corpus generalisation, EER.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
Self-supervised (SSL) front-ends such as wav2vec\,2.0 and XLS-R, paired with
lightweight back-ends, reach very low in-domain error on standard anti-spoofing
benchmarks~\cite{wav2vec2aug,xlsrsls,aasist}. Deployed on audio from a
different source---new microphones, codecs, languages, or generators---accuracy
drops steeply, a well-documented generalisation gap~\cite{asdg,crossdomain,itw}.

We start from a diagnostic measurement: a fine-tuned XLS-R detector evaluated on
an unseen corpus keeps a high \textbf{ROC-AUC while its accuracy at a fixed
threshold collapses}. It still \emph{ranks} clips correctly; its operating point
is mis-calibrated. Because ranking transfers, the most-confident predictions on
unlabeled target audio are trustworthy enough to adapt on at test time.

That observation is the starting point of a family of methods, not a method.
The step every member of the family takes---label the extreme quantiles of the
target scores and self-train on them---carries an assumption that is invisible
on the benchmarks the field uses and fatal on the pools it deploys to: that the
target pool is roughly class-balanced. Take the top and bottom $30\%$ of a
$97\%$-spoof pool and the ``confidently bona fide'' bucket is mechanically made
of low-scoring \emph{spoof}. Self-training then teaches the detector that a
great deal of spoof is genuine. This is not hypothetical: it is what happens to all three released
state-of-the-art checkpoints we test on the official ASVspoof2021-DF protocol
(Table~\ref{tab:protoa}), and to three of four modern TTA baselines.

\textbf{Contributions.} (i) The ranking--calibration asymmetry as the mechanism
of cross-corpus failure, over $369$ third-party cells and tested directly: a
proxy $\mathcal{A}$-distance \emph{rises} after adaptation, so the effect is
operating-point repair, not learned invariance. (ii) The class-prior assumption
hidden in confident-quantile TTA and in three of four modern TTA baselines,
measured on the field's standard benchmark at its real prevalence, where it
destroys the operating point of three released SOTA checkpoints. (iii) A
label-free correction---shift estimation of the target prior, used to split the
pseudo-label budget---which removes the damage on every DF checkpoint,
threshold-free, together with the identifiability argument that bounds when it
can be trusted. (iv) Two label-free monitors that separate
damaging adaptations from safe ones, including our own published configuration.

\textbf{Related work.} SSL front-ends with AASIST or attentive back-ends
dominate recent benchmarks~\cite{wav2vec2aug,xlsrsls,aasist,rawnet2}. Train-time
domain generalisation aligns source domains~\cite{asdg} or runs gradient
reversal over source and target jointly~\cite{dann}; both act before deployment.
In TTA, entropy minimisation (Tent)~\cite{tent} is the reference recipe, and its
instability prompted reliable-sample filtering (ETA/EATA)~\cite{eata},
sharpness-aware entropy with a recovery scheme (SAR)~\cite{sar}, and information
maximisation with self-labelling (SHOT)~\cite{shot} --- we compare against all
four. Batch-statistics recalibration~\cite{adabn} is a no-op for LayerNorm.
The prior estimator is black-box shift estimation~\cite{bbse}; the proxy
$\mathcal{A}$-distance is from~\cite{bendavid}.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
Let $x$ be a waveform with label $y\in\{0,1\}$ (fake$=1$) and let $f_\theta$
produce $s_\theta(x)=P_\theta(y{=}1\mid x)$. We are given a source model, its
labelled training corpus $\mathcal{S}$, and an \emph{unlabelled} target pool
$\mathcal{T}$. No target label is read at any point.

\textbf{What breaks under corpus shift.} Ranking quality
$\mathrm{AUC}=\Pr[s_\theta(x^+){>}s_\theta(x^-)]$ is threshold-free; accuracy at
a fixed $\tau$ is not. Empirically AUC degrades far less than accuracy at
$\tau{=}0.5$ under source$\to$target shift: across $369$ third-party evaluation
cells the accuracy a model's ranking supports but its shipped threshold does not
deliver averages $7.29$ points. The deployable object is therefore the score
\emph{order}, and the broken object is the operating point. Everything below follows from taking that literally.

\textbf{(a) The target prior, label-free.} An operating point is a statement
about a prior, so we estimate the prior rather than assume it. Black-box shift
estimation~\cite{bbse} gives $\hat p = M^{-\top}q$, where
$M[y,\hat y]=P_{\mathcal S}(\hat y\mid y)$ is the source confusion matrix
measured on the model's own labelled training data and $q$ is the observed
target prediction histogram. Estimators split into two families and the split
matters: BBSE and EM~\cite{sld} read the model's \emph{thresholded prediction
rate}, a two-component Gaussian mixture reads the \emph{shape} of the score
histogram. The first inherits whatever calibration error the target induces ---
on In-the-Wild, where the checkpoint predicts $58.9\%$ fake on a $37.2\%$-fake
pool, BBSE returns $0.600$ and EM $0.614$ while the mixture returns $0.422$; on
the well-calibrated DF pool all three agree to within $0.02$
(Sec.~\ref{sec:lim}). We report DF with BBSE as run, a mixture prior giving a
near-identical budget there.

\textbf{(b) The tail budget, and when it is valid.} The standard rule takes the
bottom $q$ and top $q$ of the target scores as pseudo-real and pseudo-fake. Its validity has a characterisation that
needs no model at all. A bucket of $qN$ clips can contain at most the pool's
$\pi_{\mathrm{real}}N$ real clips, so \emph{for any detector whatsoever}
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{purity}(q)\;\le\;\min\!\big(1,\ \pi_{\mathrm{real}}/q\big),
\label{eq:purity}
\end{equation}
and symmetrically for the fake tail --- so the rule can be sound only while
\begin{equation}
q\;\le\;\min(\pi,\,1-\pi).
\label{eq:valid}
\end{equation}
Eq.~\eqref{eq:purity} is counting, not an approximation; what is empirical is
its \emph{tightness}, which is where the ranking precondition enters. Across all
seven (checkpoint, corpus) cells here the bound is never violated and is attained
to within $0.025$, the slack tracking AUC ($0.000$ at AUC $.985$, $0.025$ at
$.917$). Eq.~\eqref{eq:valid} then accounts for every result we report: at
$q{=}0.3$ In-the-Wild satisfies it ($.372{>}.3$, purity $.94$--$.99$) and the
recipe repairs checkpoints, while on DF ($\pi_{\mathrm{real}}{=}.028$) no
detector can exceed $9.3\%$ purity --- we measure $8.6\%$, i.e.\ $92\%$ of the
``confidently bona fide'' labels are spoof --- and the recipe destroys them. The
published configuration cannot work at that prevalence, whatever the model.

Two fixes follow, and they differ in what they assume. Splitting the same budget
$2q$ by an estimated prior,
$q_{\mathrm{real}}{=}2q\hat\pi_{\mathrm{real}}$,
$q_{\mathrm{fake}}{=}2q(1{-}\hat\pi_{\mathrm{real}})$, restores
Eq.~\eqref{eq:valid} and is \emph{identical} to the symmetric rule at
$\hat\pi{=}0.5$, so it is provably inert on balanced pools; but it needs $\pi$,
which Sec.~\ref{sec:lim} shows is not identifiable. Alternatively $q$ is
\emph{ours to choose} while $\pi$ is not: shrinking $q$ below the most skewed
prior anticipated satisfies Eq.~\eqref{eq:valid} without estimating anything
($q{=}0.02$ holds purity ${\ge}0.956$ on all seven cells). We report both.
A fixed \emph{confidence} threshold is a third option that only moves the
problem --- prior-free but calibration-dependent, and calibration is what this
section measures as not transferring --- so we run it as a baseline rather than
argue about it.

\textbf{(c) Objective.} With pseudo-labels $\tilde y$ from (b) on the confident
set $\mathcal{C}$ and a stochastic channel perturbation $a(\cdot)$ (per-clip
gain $g\sim\mathcal U(0.7,1.3)$ plus Gaussian noise $\sigma{=}0.005$),
\begin{equation}
\min_{\{\gamma,\beta\},\phi}\
\underbrace{\textstyle\sum_{x\in\mathcal C}\!\mathrm{CE}\big(f_\theta(x),\tilde y\big)}_{\mathcal L_{\mathrm{ST}}}
+\ \lambda\,\underbrace{\big\|f_\theta(a(x))-\mathrm{sg}[f_\theta(x)]\big\|^2}_{\mathcal L_{\mathrm{cons}}},
\end{equation}
optimising only the LayerNorm affine parameters $\{\gamma,\beta\}$ of the
fine-tuned blocks and the head $\phi$ ($16{,}706$ parameters), with
$\lambda{=}0.3$, $q{=}0.3$. Restricting the trainable set and using only
prior-corrected confident tails is what prevents the degenerate collapse to
which entropy minimisation is prone.

\textbf{(d) Two label-free guards.} Adaptation can damage the model it is
given, so a deployer needs to decide --- without labels --- whether to keep the
adapted weights. Since the source ranking is the asset being exploited, we
compare the adapted scores' order against the frozen source model's,
$\rho=\mathrm{Spearman}\big(s_{\theta}(\mathcal T),s_{\theta_0}(\mathcal T)\big)$,
and separately track the drift of the predicted positive rate from
$\hat\pi$. We use these \emph{post hoc}, as a keep/abort decision on a completed
adaptation; a per-step stopping rule is the natural extension and we do not
claim it here.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\textbf{Protocol A (published baselines exist).} The official ASVspoof2021-DF
eval partition against the official CM keys; $400{,}435$ of $611{,}829$ trials are
held locally, the missing part uniform across codec, label and phase (verified
$75$--$25$ in every stratum), true spoof rate $97.22\%$. For scale, published pooled EERs on this protocol run from $1.95$
(Wav2DF-TSL) and $2.87$ (SSL$+$SLS) through $8.54$ (Wav2Vec2-AASIST) to $15.64$
(challenge DF top-1); our own source model reaches only $26.33$, so we run the
method on \emph{released} checkpoints instead:
DeepFense XLS-R\,300M\,+\,AASIST trained on ASVspoof2019-LA~\cite{deepfensehf}, at
three independent training seeds. Adaptation uses an unlabelled $20{,}000$-clip
subsample of the eval pool; we report the full official pooled EER and, as an
inductive check, the EER over trials \emph{not} in that subsample.

\textbf{Protocol B (cross-corpus, balanced).} Leave-one-corpus-out over four
EER-capable targets: ASVspoof2019-LA~\cite{asvspoof19}; D2 (LibriSpeech bona
fide vs.\ eight open TTS systems); In-the-Wild~\cite{itw}; and the Arabic Audio
Deepfake set~\cite{arad}. Each held-out target's source pool merges the other
three with a 38-language MLAAD subset~\cite{mlaad}; held-out target audio is
never in any source pool and six MLAAD languages are held out of every pool.
Ten seeds per target per method.

\textbf{Protocol C (a benchmark at its own prevalence).} All $31{,}779$
In-the-Wild clips at their native $37.2\%$ fake rate --- skewed the
\emph{opposite} way to DF's $97.2\%$.

\textbf{Comparison points.} Source-only; the median-threshold rule; Tent, SHOT,
ETA/EATA, SAR; AdaBN~\cite{adabn}; DANN~\cite{dann} (trained to convergence with
the held-out target's unlabeled inputs --- more privileged access than ours); an
ASDG-style baseline~\cite{asdg}; and a target-supervised oracle. Every TTA arm adapts
the same $16{,}706$ parameters with the same lr, batch and pass count; none was
tuned separately, and none could be, since per-target TTA tuning needs the
target labels the setting assumes absent. Two published constants in the modern
TTA
baselines are degenerate at $C{=}2$ and were re-derived rather than
transplanted. EATA's redundancy filter ($|\cos(p,\bar p)|{<}0.05$) is calibrated
for $C{=}1000$; on the binary simplex the minimum attainable cosine is $0.71$ at
$\bar p{=}(.5,.5)$ and $0.11$ at $(.9,.1)$, so nothing passes until the running
average itself reaches a vertex, after which only \emph{minority}-predicted
samples pass --- neither is the stated mechanism, so we run the reliability
filter alone, which is EATA's own published ablation. SAR's recovery trigger
$e_0{=}0.2$ sits far below ImageNet's $E_0{=}0.4\ln 1000{=}2.76$ but
\emph{inside} the $C{=}2$ reliable band $H{<}0.277$, firing on every batch and
freezing the model at its initial weights (verified: $32$ resets in $32$ steps,
zero net movement); we keep its relative position,
$e_0=(0.2/\ln 1000)\ln C=0.020$.

\textbf{Implementation.} 16\,kHz audio, top-4-layer XLS-R fine-tuning for our own
source models (Adam, lr\,$2{\times}10^{-4}$, batch 32, 8 epochs). Adaptation:
lr\,$1{\times}10^{-4}$, $E{=}4$ passes, batch $32$ (third-party crops matched to
each checkpoint's own training length). Code and manifests:
\texttt{[URL withheld]}.

\subsection{The class prior is the hidden variable}
Table~\ref{tab:protoa} is the main result. Applied unmodified to a released
checkpoint already better than the published Wav2Vec2-AASIST baseline here, the
symmetric-quantile configuration \emph{damages} it: accuracy
$98.87\!\to\!62.96\%$, EER $4.51\!\to\!5.84\%$, manufacturing a $31$-point deficit
where there was none. Eqs.~\eqref{eq:purity}--\eqref{eq:valid} say why, and by how much.

The published baselines are no better placed, and each fails through its own
version of the assumption. SHOT minimises
$\mathbb{E}[H(p)]-H(\mathbb{E}[p])$, whose diversity term is maximised when the
mean prediction is \emph{uniform} --- at $C{=}2$ a hard-coded prior of
$\tfrac12$, asking it to move $47$ points of probability mass into the bona fide
class: the regulariser introduced to \emph{cure} Tent-style collapse encodes the
assumption more literally than Tent does. ETA fails more instructively still: its
reliability filter, meant to discard the high-entropy samples that destabilise
entropy minimisation, admitted $99.8\%$ of them, so ETA reduced to Tent and
landed worse. An entropy-keyed filter separates confident from uncertain, not
\emph{confident-and-right} from \emph{confident-and-wrong}, and mis-calibration
manufactures exactly the latter --- a safeguard keyed on the model's own
confidence inherits the broken calibration it guards against.

SAR is the exception that clarifies the rule: the only prior method that does
not degrade the checkpoint, and it manages that by declining to move it ($308$
model-recovery resets in $2{,}500$ steps, AUC unchanged to four decimals). Its
guard does what it promises, and prevention is all it delivers. So the claim is not that every published method fails here. It is
that \emph{three of the five prior configurations degrade a state-of-the-art
detector at the standard benchmark's own prevalence, the fourth avoids damage
only by reverting its own updates, and none of them improves it.}

\subsection{The failure, and where the recipe already works}
Table~\ref{tab:itw} is the same recipe at a prior that
\emph{satisfies} Eq.~\eqref{eq:valid} ($0.372>0.3$). DeepFense s42 --- the
checkpoint that scores $4.56\%$ on DF --- arrives here ranking at AUC $0.917$
while correct on $39.1\%$ of clips, fewer than a majority-class predictor.
Self-training repairs exactly that: on the three checkpoints with a deficit it
drives $\delta$ from $16.7$, $22.6$, $44.4$ to $0.1$, $2.9$, $5.0$; on the
well-calibrated one it has nothing to repair and costs a little. On s42 it also
raises \textbf{AUC $.917\!\to\!.947$} --- which no threshold can do --- with the
disjoint half slightly better ($12.27$ / $.949$), so the ranking gain is not
memorisation.

\subsection{Why this is not a re-thresholding result}
Both EER and AUC improve, and neither takes a threshold as input, so no
operating-point rule can produce this gain. The cleanest form of that argument
uses the \emph{same} prior estimate twice: re-point the threshold with it and
take no gradient step, and accuracy moves $98.92\!\to\!98.94\%$ while EER stays
at $4.51$ by construction; feed it to the pseudo-label budget instead and EER
goes to $4.02$. The prior does its work in the budget, not at the threshold.

On \emph{balanced} pools a one-line median-threshold rule is genuinely
competitive with gradient adaptation on accuracy --- we reported it as such, and
it is the right control for a calibration claim. Resampling the DF scores across
target priors shows why, and what it costs: the median rule gives $95.6\%$
accuracy at a prior of $0.5$ (shipped threshold $88.3$, labelled oracle $95.7$)
and falls monotonically to $59.0\%$ at the benchmark's own $0.972$, while a
threshold at the $(1{-}\hat\pi)$ quantile tracks the shipped one throughout.

\noindent That is the rule's assumption, not an accident --- and every cell on
which it was previously evaluated satisfied that assumption by construction,
having been balanced to a prevalence of $0.489$--$0.539$ by the evaluation
pipeline itself.

\noindent Paired over the twelve folds we beat source-only ($10/12$,
$p{=}0.027$), Tent ($p{=}0.012$), ETA ($p{=}0.003$), EATA ($p{=}0.016$) and SAR
($p{=}0.027$) --- but \textbf{not SHOT} ($17.44$ vs.\ $17.76$, $7/12$,
$p{=}0.47$). On balanced pools SHOT is our peer, and on ASVspoof2019 it is
better. That is the point rather than an embarrassment: SHOT's diversity term is
a hard uniform prior, which costs nothing when the pool is balanced and
destroys a released checkpoint when it is not ($6.38\%$ EER and $77.6\%$ accuracy
on DF, against our $4.02$ and $98.95$). The separation between the two methods
lives entirely in the regime the field does not test. Tent, ETA and EATA are
meanwhile unstable even here, at $29$--$32\%$ pooled EER against a source-only
$18.39$, and SAR is again exactly inert (pooled EER identical to source-only to
two decimals).

\subsection{Label-free guards}
Whether to keep an adapted model is itself decidable without labels. Over the
eight adapted DF arms, rank agreement $\rho$ and prediction-rate drift are
complementary --- $\rho$ catches ranking collapse (ETA $0.31$, Tent $0.44$), the
drift catches operating-point collapse ($0.37$, $0.39$, $0.23$) and is blind to
the former --- and requiring both separates the five harmful arms from the three
harmless ones exactly, for any $\tau\in[0.70,0.80]$. The plateau matters more
than the exactness: a $\rho$-only rule at $\tau{=}0.85$, fitted on seven arms,
looked equally exact and was falsified by the eighth, rejecting the study's best
result. Two thresholds on eight points is a diagnostic, not a rule;
\PENDING{stage 6/7} gives the leave-one-target-out version.

\subsection{Breadth, and the mechanism}
Applied unmodified to nine released checkpoints across five architectures and
ten corpora ($369$ cells), the consistent effect is \emph{re-calibration}:
$\delta$ falls $7.29\!\to\!1.96$ ($248/369$, $p{=}2.2{\times}10^{-17}$), and
where it lands barely depends on where it started
($\mathrm{corr}{=}{+}0.17$). The mechanism is \emph{not} learned invariance: a
proxy $\mathcal{A}$-distance and a linear domain-origin probe both \emph{rise}
after adaptation (Arabic $1.545\!\to\!1.606$), the opposite of what
domain-invariance predicts and consistent with DANN underperforming while
optimising that very quantity. Beyond any threshold, the gradient step adds
separability where the ranking is strong: on ASVspoof2019 within-attack-family
AUC rises on all six families ($30/30$, $p{=}10^{-9}$), and on ITW/s42 AUC rises
$.917\!\to\!.947$; on the AUC-$0.71$ target it is null.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:lim}
\textbf{The prior is not identifiable, which bounds the correction.} A
label-free method sees only
$\Pr[\hat y{=}1]=\pi\,\mathrm{TPR}_{\mathcal T}+(1{-}\pi)\mathrm{FPR}_{\mathcal T}$:
one equation, three unknowns. BBSE closes it by assuming source error rates
carry over --- what covariate shift breaks --- so an extreme prior and an extreme
calibration failure look identical (DF/s2 predicts $97.4\%$ fake at a true
$0.972$, ITW/s42 $98.1\%$ at a true $0.372$). The estimate therefore degrades exactly as the operating point does --- over
seven cells and two corpora, $|\hat\pi-\pi|$ against the source deficit gives
Pearson $r{=}0.998$ --- and a badly wrong prior is worse than none (ITW/s42
$12.58\!\to\!14.51$ EER). Estimating the prior and detecting that the estimate is
wrong are one problem; two label-free detectors for it are falsified in our
release.


\textbf{Scope.} The prior fixes the \emph{budget}, not the threshold; the gain
varies ($1.27$ to $0.02$ EER across DF checkpoints); and a low-AUC start we
cannot rescue at all.
