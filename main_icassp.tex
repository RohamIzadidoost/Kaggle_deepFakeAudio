\begin{abstract}
Audio deepfake detectors generalise poorly across corpora despite near-perfect
in-domain accuracy. We show the failure is one of \emph{operating point}: a
fine-tuned self-supervised detector's \emph{ranking} (ROC-AUC) largely transfers
cross-corpus while its decision \emph{threshold} does not, so unlabeled target
audio can repair the operating point at test time. We further show that
confident-quantile test-time adaptation (TTA) silently assumes a
\emph{class-balanced} target pool --- an assumption that holds where TTA is
measured and essentially nowhere else, in either direction: the standard
ASVspoof2021-DF benchmark is $97.2\%$ spoof, In-the-Wild is $37.2\%$. On the
official DF eval ($400{,}435$ trials) the published symmetric-quantile
configuration destroys the operating point of three independently trained
state-of-the-art released checkpoints, driving accuracy from $98.8$--$99.1\%$ to
$62$--$65\%$; three of four modern TTA baselines (Tent, SHOT, ETA) degrade them
too, each through its own version of the same assumption, while SAR avoids
damage only by reverting its own updates. Correcting the pseudo-label budget
with a label-free estimate of the target prior ($\hat\pi{=}0.9715$ vs.\ a true
$0.9708$) instead preserves accuracy and improves EER on all three, beating the
symmetric budget by $0.79$--$1.82$ points and the source model by up to $27.9\%$
relative. Because EER and AUC both move, no re-thresholding rule accounts for
it: the one-line median-threshold control that looks competitive on balanced
pools loses $40$ accuracy points at this benchmark's real prior. Two label-free
monitors --- rank agreement with the frozen source model, and prediction-rate
drift --- separate the adaptations that damaged a checkpoint from those that did
not.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
audio deepfake detection, anti-spoofing, test-time adaptation, label shift,
cross-corpus generalisation, EER.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
Self-supervised (SSL) front-ends such as wav2vec\,2.0 and XLS-R, paired with
lightweight back-ends, reach very low in-domain error on standard anti-spoofing
benchmarks~\cite{wav2vec2aug,xlsrsls,aasist}. Deployed on audio from a
different source---new microphones, codecs, languages, or generators---accuracy
drops steeply, a well-documented generalisation gap~\cite{asdg,crossdomain,itw}.

We start from a diagnostic measurement: a fine-tuned XLS-R detector evaluated on
an unseen corpus keeps a high \textbf{ROC-AUC while its accuracy at a fixed
threshold collapses}. It still \emph{ranks} clips correctly; its operating point
is mis-calibrated. Because ranking transfers, the most-confident predictions on
unlabeled target audio are trustworthy enough to adapt on at test time.

That observation is the starting point of a family of methods, not a method.
The step every member of the family takes---label the extreme quantiles of the
target scores and self-train on them---carries an assumption that is invisible
on the benchmarks the field uses and fatal on the pools it deploys to: that the
target pool is roughly class-balanced. Take the top and bottom $30\%$ of a
$97\%$-spoof pool and the ``confidently bona fide'' bucket is mechanically made
of low-scoring \emph{spoof}. Self-training then teaches the detector that a
great deal of spoof is genuine. This is not hypothetical: it is what happens to
a released state-of-the-art checkpoint on the official ASVspoof2021-DF
protocol (Table~\ref{tab:protoa}).

\textbf{Contributions.} (i) The ranking--calibration asymmetry as the mechanism
of cross-corpus failure, over $369$ third-party cells and tested directly: a
proxy $\mathcal{A}$-distance \emph{rises} after adaptation, so the effect is
operating-point repair, not learned invariance. (ii) The class-prior assumption
hidden in confident-quantile TTA and in three of four modern TTA baselines,
measured on the field's standard benchmark at its real prevalence, where it
destroys the operating point of three released SOTA checkpoints. (iii) A
label-free correction---shift estimation of the target prior, used to split the
pseudo-label budget---which removes the damage and beats the symmetric budget on
every checkpoint, threshold-free. (iv) Two label-free monitors that separate
damaging adaptations from safe ones, including our own published configuration.

\textbf{Related work.} SSL front-ends with AASIST or attentive back-ends
dominate recent benchmarks~\cite{wav2vec2aug,xlsrsls,aasist,rawnet2}. Train-time
domain generalisation aligns source domains~\cite{asdg} or runs gradient
reversal over source and target jointly~\cite{dann}; both act before deployment.
In TTA, entropy minimisation (Tent)~\cite{tent} is the reference recipe, and its
instability prompted reliable-sample filtering (ETA/EATA)~\cite{eata},
sharpness-aware entropy with a recovery scheme (SAR)~\cite{sar}, and information
maximisation with self-labelling (SHOT)~\cite{shot} --- we compare against all
four. Batch-statistics recalibration~\cite{adabn} is a no-op for LayerNorm.
The prior estimator is black-box shift estimation~\cite{bbse}; the proxy
$\mathcal{A}$-distance is from~\cite{bendavid}.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
Let $x$ be a waveform with label $y\in\{0,1\}$ (fake$=1$) and let $f_\theta$
produce $s_\theta(x)=P_\theta(y{=}1\mid x)$. We are given a source model, its
labelled training corpus $\mathcal{S}$, and an \emph{unlabelled} target pool
$\mathcal{T}$. No target label is read at any point.

\textbf{What breaks under corpus shift.} Ranking quality
$\mathrm{AUC}=\Pr[s_\theta(x^+){>}s_\theta(x^-)]$ is threshold-free; accuracy at
a fixed $\tau$ is not. Empirically AUC degrades far less than accuracy at
$\tau{=}0.5$ under source$\to$target shift: across $369$ third-party evaluation
cells the accuracy a model's ranking supports but its shipped threshold does not
deliver averages $7.29$ points. The deployable object is therefore the score
\emph{order}, and the broken object is the operating point. Everything below follows from taking that literally.

\textbf{(a) The target prior, label-free.} An operating point is a statement
about a prior, so we estimate the prior rather than assume it. Black-box shift
estimation~\cite{bbse} gives $\hat p = M^{-\top}q$, where
$M[y,\hat y]=P_{\mathcal S}(\hat y\mid y)$ is the source confusion matrix
measured on the model's own labelled training data and $q$ is the observed
target prediction histogram. Label-free prior estimators divide into two families, and the
split matters more than we expected. BBSE and EM~\cite{sld} read the model's
\emph{thresholded prediction rate} on the target; a two-component Gaussian
mixture reads the \emph{shape} of the target score histogram. The first family inherits
whatever calibration error the target induces --- exactly the quantity this paper
says does not transfer. On In-the-Wild, where the checkpoint predicts $58.9\%$
fake on a $37.2\%$-fake pool, BBSE returns $\hat\pi{=}0.600$ and EM $0.614$ while
the mixture returns $0.422$; on the well-calibrated DF pool all three agree to
within $0.02$. A prediction-rate estimator is accurate where the operating point
is already sound and unreliable where it is not. We report DF with BBSE as run
(a mixture prior gives a near-identical budget there); on a badly mis-calibrated
pool the mixture is the safer default. What matters most is estimating the prior at all rather
than assuming it is $\tfrac12$.

\textbf{(b) Prior-corrected confident tails.} The standard rule takes the bottom
$q$ and top $q$ of the target scores as pseudo-real and pseudo-fake. We split the
same total budget $2q$ by the estimated prior,
\begin{equation}
q_{\mathrm{real}} = 2q\,\hat\pi_{\mathrm{real}},\qquad
q_{\mathrm{fake}} = 2q\,(1-\hat\pi_{\mathrm{real}}),
\label{eq:budget}
\end{equation}
which is \emph{identical} to the symmetric rule at $\hat\pi_{\mathrm{real}}=0.5$
--- so on a balanced pool the correction is provably inert and cannot be the
source of any balanced-pool result --- and shrinks the pseudo-real bucket to the
estimated real fraction otherwise. The prior is estimated \emph{once}, from the
frozen pre-adaptation model: re-estimating each epoch lets a drifting model
reinforce its own mistakes.

The obvious prior-free alternative is a fixed \emph{confidence} threshold
($p\ge 0.95\Rightarrow$ fake) instead of a quantile. It does not remove the
problem, it moves it: a quantile rule is calibration-free but assumes the prior,
a confidence rule is prior-free but assumes calibration --- and calibration is
exactly the property the first paragraph of this section measures as \emph{not}
transferring across corpora. We run it as a baseline
(Table~\ref{tab:protoa}) rather than argue about it.

\textbf{(c) Objective.} With pseudo-labels $\tilde y$ from (b) on the confident
set $\mathcal{C}$ and a stochastic channel perturbation $a(\cdot)$ (per-clip
gain $g\sim\mathcal U(0.7,1.3)$ plus Gaussian noise $\sigma{=}0.005$),
\begin{equation}
\min_{\{\gamma,\beta\},\phi}\
\underbrace{\textstyle\sum_{x\in\mathcal C}\!\mathrm{CE}\big(f_\theta(x),\tilde y\big)}_{\mathcal L_{\mathrm{ST}}}
+\ \lambda\,\underbrace{\big\|f_\theta(a(x))-\mathrm{sg}[f_\theta(x)]\big\|^2}_{\mathcal L_{\mathrm{cons}}},
\end{equation}
optimising only the LayerNorm affine parameters $\{\gamma,\beta\}$ of the
fine-tuned blocks and the head $\phi$ ($16{,}706$ parameters), with
$\lambda{=}0.3$, $q{=}0.3$. Restricting the trainable set and using only
prior-corrected confident tails is what prevents the degenerate collapse to
which entropy minimisation is prone.

\textbf{(d) A label-free guard.} Adaptation needs a stop, and the step count has
been the weakest-specified part of TTA recipes here. Since the source ranking is
the asset being exploited, we monitor rank agreement with the frozen source
model,
$\rho_t=\mathrm{Spearman}\big(s_{\theta_t}(\mathcal T),s_{\theta_0}(\mathcal T)\big)$,
and keep the last step with $\rho_t\ge\tau$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:results}).

\section{Experimental Setup}
\textbf{Protocol A (published baselines exist).} The official ASVspoof2021-DF
eval partition against the official CM keys: $400{,}435$ of $611{,}829$ trials
are held locally and the missing part is uniform across codec, label and phase
(verified $75$--$25$ in every stratum), so the pooled EER is unbiased; true spoof
rate $97.22\%$. Because our own source model is not competitive here ($26.33\%$
vs.\ a published $8.54\%$), we run the method on \emph{released} checkpoints:
DeepFense XLS-R\,300M\,+\,AASIST trained on ASVspoof2019-LA~\cite{deepfensehf}, at
three independent training seeds. Adaptation uses an unlabelled $20{,}000$-clip
subsample of the eval pool; we report the full official pooled EER and, as an
inductive check, the EER over trials \emph{not} in that subsample.

\textbf{Protocol B (cross-corpus, balanced).} Leave-one-corpus-out over four
EER-capable targets: ASVspoof2019-LA~\cite{asvspoof19}; D2 (LibriSpeech bona
fide vs.\ eight open TTS systems); In-the-Wild~\cite{itw}; and the Arabic Audio
Deepfake set~\cite{arad}. Each held-out target's source pool merges the other
three with a 38-language MLAAD subset~\cite{mlaad}; held-out target audio is
never in any source pool and six MLAAD languages are held out of every pool.
Ten seeds per target per method.

\textbf{Protocol C (a benchmark at its own prevalence).} All $31{,}779$
In-the-Wild clips at their native $37.2\%$ fake rate --- skewed the
\emph{opposite} way to DF's $97.2\%$.

\textbf{Comparison points.} Source-only; the median-threshold rule; Tent, SHOT,
ETA/EATA, SAR; AdaBN~\cite{adabn}; DANN~\cite{dann} (trained to convergence with
the held-out target's unlabeled inputs --- more privileged access than ours); an
ASDG-style baseline~\cite{asdg}; and a target-supervised oracle. Every TTA arm adapts
the same $16{,}706$ parameters with the same lr, batch and pass count; none was
tuned separately, and none could be, since per-target TTA tuning needs the
target labels the setting assumes absent. Two published constants in the modern
TTA
baselines are degenerate at $C{=}2$ and were re-derived rather than
transplanted. EATA's redundancy filter ($|\cos(p,\bar p)|{<}0.05$) is calibrated
for $C{=}1000$; on the binary simplex the minimum attainable cosine is $0.71$ at
$\bar p{=}(.5,.5)$ and $0.11$ at $(.9,.1)$, so nothing passes until the running
average itself reaches a vertex, after which only \emph{minority}-predicted
samples pass --- neither is the stated mechanism, so we run the reliability
filter alone, which is EATA's own published ablation. SAR's recovery trigger
$e_0{=}0.2$ sits far below ImageNet's $E_0{=}0.4\ln 1000{=}2.76$ but
\emph{inside} the $C{=}2$ reliable band $H{<}0.277$, firing on every batch and
freezing the model at its initial weights (verified: $32$ resets in $32$ steps,
zero net movement); we keep its relative position,
$e_0=(0.2/\ln 1000)\ln C=0.020$.

\textbf{Implementation.} 16\,kHz audio, top-4-layer XLS-R fine-tuning for our own
source models (Adam, lr\,$2{\times}10^{-4}$, batch 32, 8 epochs). Adaptation:
lr\,$1{\times}10^{-4}$, $E{=}4$ passes, batch $32$ (third-party crops matched to
each checkpoint's own training length). Code and manifests:
\texttt{[URL withheld]}.

\subsection{The class prior is the hidden variable}
Table~\ref{tab:protoa} is the main result. Applied unmodified to a released
checkpoint that is already better than the published Wav2Vec2-AASIST baseline
on this protocol, the symmetric-quantile configuration \emph{damages} it:
accuracy $98.87\!\to\!62.96\%$, EER $4.51\!\to\!5.84\%$, and it manufactures a
$31$-point calibration deficit where there was none. The mechanism is
mechanical: $q{=}0.3$ labels the bottom $30\%$ of a $97.2\%$-spoof pool
``confidently bona fide''.

The published baselines are no better placed, and one states the assumption
outright: SHOT minimises $\mathbb{E}[H(p)]-H(\mathbb{E}[p])$, whose diversity
term is maximised when the mean prediction is \emph{uniform} --- at $C{=}2$ a
hard-coded prior of $\tfrac12$, asking it to move $47$ points of probability mass
into the bona fide class. It degrades the checkpoint accordingly, with no score
saturation to blame. The regulariser introduced to \emph{cure} Tent-style
collapse encodes the assumption more literally than Tent does.

ETA fails more instructively. Its reliability filter exists to discard the
high-entropy samples that destabilise entropy minimisation; here it admitted
$99.8\%$ of them, so ETA reduced to Tent and landed worse (AUC $.9288$, the
largest ranking loss of any arm). That is structural: an entropy-keyed filter
separates confident from uncertain, not \emph{confident-and-right} from
\emph{confident-and-wrong}, and mis-calibration manufactures exactly the latter.
A safeguard keyed on the model's own confidence inherits the broken calibration
it guards against.

SAR is the exception that clarifies the rule. It is the only prior method here
that does not degrade the checkpoint --- and it manages that by declining to
move it: $308$ model-recovery resets in $2{,}500$ steps, an AUC unchanged to
four decimals, and a score distribution indistinguishable from the source
model's. Its collapse guard does exactly what it promises, and prevention is all
it delivers. So the claim is not that every published method fails here. It is
that \emph{three of the five prior configurations degrade a state-of-the-art
detector at the standard benchmark's own prevalence, the fourth avoids damage
only by reverting its own updates, and none of them improves it.}

Replacing the symmetric budget by Eq.~\eqref{eq:budget} with a label-free prior
estimate reverses this. BBSE returns $\hat\pi_{\mathrm{fake}}{=}0.9715$ against a
true $0.9708$, giving a budget of $(0.017,0.583)$ instead of $(0.3,0.3)$, and EER
improves to $4.02\%$ --- with no target labels and no retraining. The change
between the two arms is one scalar.

\noindent Two things replicate and one does not, and the distinction is the

replicates on all three (accuracy $62$--$65\%$, deficit $+30$ to $+34$), and

mis-specified prior reliably breaks the operating point, not the ranking.
Against source-only, prior correction improves EER on $3/3$ but by very
different amounts ($1.27$, $0.49$, $0.02$; AUC up on two, flat on the third), so
we rest on the unanimous controlled comparison and report the against-source
magnitudes as varying.

\subsection{Why this is not a re-thresholding result}
Both EER and AUC improve, and neither takes a threshold as input, so no
operating-point rule can produce this gain. The cleanest form of that argument
uses the \emph{same} prior estimate twice: re-point the threshold with it and
take no gradient step, and accuracy moves $98.92\!\to\!98.94\%$ while EER stays
at $4.51$ by construction; feed it to the pseudo-label budget instead and EER
goes to $4.02$. The prior does its work in the budget, not at the threshold.

On \emph{balanced} pools a one-line median-threshold rule is genuinely
competitive with gradient adaptation on accuracy --- we reported it as such, and
it is the right control for a calibration claim. Resampling the DF scores across
target priors shows why, and what it costs: the median rule gives $95.6\%$
accuracy at a prior of $0.5$ (shipped threshold $88.3$, labelled oracle $95.7$)
and falls monotonically to $59.0\%$ at the benchmark's own $0.972$, while a
threshold at the $(1{-}\hat\pi)$ quantile tracks the shipped one throughout.

\noindent That is the rule's assumption, not an accident --- and every cell on
which it was previously evaluated satisfied that assumption by construction,
having been balanced to a prevalence of $0.489$--$0.539$ by the evaluation
pipeline itself.

\textbf{Tent is unpredictably catastrophic, and sometimes silently so.} On
ASVspoof2019 its per-seed EER splits five seeds near source-only and four at or
near chance with no warning sign (std $21.96$); on Arabic it is a consistent
chance-level failure ($9/10$ seeds). Ours shows no comparable collapse anywhere
(largest std $2.01$). The failure is a ranking failure first: on the strongest
and weakest public checkpoints Tent collapses to chance on one
($16.41\!\to\!49.80\%$ EER, AUC $.918\!\to\!.502$) but only degrades AUC on the
other. On the DF pool the damage does not show in the field's headline metric at
all --- Tent returns a \emph{lower} EER than source ($4.06$ vs.\ $4.51$) while
saturating $98\%$ of the pool to one tied score. A TTA study reporting EER
without a score-resolution check can record a collapse as an improvement.

\subsection{Label-free guards}
If the source \emph{ranking} is the asset adaptation exploits, an update that
destroys it has discarded its own precondition --- and that is visible without
labels, by comparing the adapted scores' \emph{order} against the frozen source
model's ($\rho$, Table~\ref{tab:protoa}). A second monitor, the drift of the
predicted positive rate from $\hat\pi$, sees the other failure. Over the eight adapted arms
here the two are complementary and both are needed: $\rho$ catches ranking
collapse decisively (ETA $0.31$, Tent $0.44$) but only marginally the
operating-point ones, while the gap catches those decisively ($0.37$, $0.39$,
$0.23$) and is blind to ranking collapse ($0.007$ for ETA). Requiring both ---
$\rho\ge\tau$ and gap $\le0.05$ --- separates the five harmful arms from the three
harmless ones exactly, for any $\tau\in[0.70,0.80]$.

The plateau matters more than the exactness, which is fragile: an earlier
$\rho$-only rule at $\tau{=}0.85$, fitted on the first seven arms, looked equally
exact and was falsified by the eighth --- it rejected the best result in this
study (seed 42, $\rho{=}0.828$, EER $4.56\!\to\!3.29$). Two thresholds fitted on
eight points is a diagnostic, not a validated rule.
\PENDING{stage 6/7} gives the out-of-sample version: per-epoch traces on the four
leave-one-corpus-out targets, thresholds chosen leave-one-target-out against the
oracle budget, with churn and confidence as controls.

\subsection{Breadth, and the mechanism}
Applied unmodified to nine released checkpoints across five architectures and
ten corpora ($369$ cells), the consistent effect is \emph{re-calibration}:
$\delta$ falls $7.29\!\to\!1.96$ points ($248/369$,
$p{=}2.2{\times}10^{-17}$), with
$\mathrm{corr}(\delta_{\text{before}},\delta_{\text{after}}){=}{+}0.17$ --- where
it lands barely depends on where it started --- and the same rule designed
against XLS-R re-calibrates a mel-spectrogram transformer~\cite{ast} unchanged
($39/39$, $p{=}5{\times}10^{-8}$). The mechanism is \emph{not} learned
invariance: a proxy $\mathcal{A}$-distance and a linear domain-origin probe both
\emph{rise} after adaptation (Arabic $1.545\!\to\!1.606$), the opposite of what
domain-invariance predicts and consistent with DANN underperforming while
optimising that very quantity. What the gradient adds beyond any threshold is
separability where the ranking is strong: on ASVspoof2019 it raises
within-attack-family AUC on all six families ($30/30$, $p{=}10^{-9}$); on
AUC-$0.71$ LibriSpeech-TTS it is null.

\section{Limitations and Conclusion}
\label{sec:lim}
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}\setlength{\topsep}{2pt}
\item \textbf{Prior estimation is the weakest link and fails where it is most
needed.} Estimators reading the thresholded prediction rate inherit the target's
calibration error: on In-the-Wild BBSE is off by $+0.23$, a score mixture by
$+0.05$. The correction still did no harm there ($4.54$ vs a source $5.06$), but
that is a property of this pool, not a guarantee. The prior also fixes the
budget rather than the threshold, and the gain's size varies a great deal across
checkpoints ($1.27$ to $0.02$ EER points).
\item \textbf{Needs a reliable source ranking, and improves someone else's
model.} A low-AUC start is not a regime we can rescue (the AUC-$0.71$ target is
the one null in Table~\ref{tab:main}); and since we did not train a
Protocol-A-competitive detector, every claim here is of the form ``this wrapper
improves that detector''.
\end{itemize}
\vspace{-2pt}
\noindent In sum: cross-corpus detection fails at the operating point, the
repair needs no labels, and the recipes used assume a balance the benchmarks
have and deployments lack.
