\begin{abstract}
Audio deepfake detectors generalise poorly across corpora. We show the failure
is one of \emph{operating point} --- ranking (ROC-AUC) largely transfers while the
decision threshold does not --- and that the confident-quantile test-time
adaptation (TTA) recipes built on this can be sound only while their tail budget
satisfies $q\le\min(\pi,1{-}\pi)$: a counting bound caps pseudo-label purity at
$\min(1,\pi_{\mathrm{real}}/q)$ for \emph{any} detector. That holds where TTA is
measured and almost nowhere else. On the official ASVspoof2021-DF eval
($97.2\%$ spoof) the published $q{=}0.3$ therefore cannot exceed $9.3\%$ purity,
and it drives three strong publicly released checkpoints from $98.8$--$99.1\%$
accuracy to $62$--$65\%$; three of four modern TTA baselines fail likewise.
Resampling In-the-Wild's prevalence reproduces the collapse on demand.
Correcting the budget with a label-free prior estimate restores all three
($9/9$ paired cells, $p{=}0.004$), but the prior is not identifiable from a
model's own outputs, so the estimate degrades exactly as the operating point
does ($r{=}0.997$) --- while simply shrinking $q$ needs no estimate at all.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
audio deepfake detection, anti-spoofing, test-time adaptation, label shift,
cross-corpus generalisation, EER.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
Self-supervised (SSL) front-ends such as wav2vec\,2.0 and XLS-R, paired with
lightweight back-ends, reach very low in-domain error on standard anti-spoofing
benchmarks~\cite{wav2vec2aug,xlsrsls,aasist}. Deployed on audio from a
different source---new microphones, codecs, languages, or generators---accuracy
drops steeply, a well-documented generalisation gap~\cite{asdg,crossdomain,itw}.

We start from a diagnostic measurement: a fine-tuned XLS-R detector evaluated on
an unseen corpus keeps a high \textbf{ROC-AUC while its accuracy at a fixed
threshold collapses}. It still \emph{ranks} clips correctly; its operating point
is mis-calibrated. Because ranking transfers, the most-confident predictions on
unlabeled target audio are trustworthy enough to adapt on at test time.

That observation is the starting point of a family of methods, not a method.
The step every member of the family takes---label the extreme quantiles of the
target scores and self-train on them---carries an assumption that is invisible
on the benchmarks the field uses and fatal on the pools it deploys to: that the
target pool is roughly class-balanced. Take the top and bottom $30\%$ of a
$97\%$-spoof pool and the ``confidently bona fide'' bucket is mechanically made
of low-scoring \emph{spoof}. Self-training then teaches the detector that a
great deal of spoof is genuine. This is not hypothetical: it is what happens to all three released
publicly released checkpoints we test on the official ASVspoof2021-DF protocol
(Table~\ref{tab:protoa}), and to three of four modern TTA baselines.

\textbf{Contributions.} (i) The ranking--calibration asymmetry as the mechanism
of cross-corpus failure, over $369$ third-party cells and tested directly: a
proxy $\mathcal{A}$-distance \emph{rises} after adaptation, so the effect is
operating-point repair, not learned invariance. (ii) An exact \emph{necessary}
prevalence condition for the confident-quantile rule, $q\le\min(\pi,1{-}\pi)$,
from a counting bound on pseudo-label purity; it accounts for every result here
and is confirmed by intervening on prevalence directly. (iii) A label-free
correction that removes the damage on every DF checkpoint, with an
identifiability argument bounding when it can be trusted --- and an oracle-prior
arm showing that what fails is the estimate, not the correction. (iv) Two
label-free monitors, one of which recovers $13.9$ EER points from a Tent
collapse out-of-sample, together with the precondition they themselves require.

\textbf{Related work.} SSL front-ends with AASIST or attentive back-ends dominate
recent benchmarks~\cite{wav2vec2aug,xlsrsls,aasist,rawnet2}. Train-time domain
generalisation aligns source domains~\cite{asdg} or runs gradient reversal over
source and target jointly~\cite{dann}; both act before deployment. In TTA,
entropy minimisation (Tent)~\cite{tent} is the reference recipe, and its
instability prompted reliable-sample filtering (ETA/EATA)~\cite{eata},
sharpness-aware entropy with recovery (SAR)~\cite{sar}, and information maximisation with
self-labelling (SHOT)~\cite{shot} --- we compare against all four. Closest to us
is T$^2$A~\cite{t2a}, which likewise targets TTA \emph{for deepfake detection}
and likewise diagnoses entropy minimisation's confirmation bias, replacing it
with uncertainty-aware negative learning; it is evaluated on image/video
deepfakes and does not consider the target class prior, which is the axis this
paper isolates. Our findings are complementary: T$^2$A repairs the objective,
we show that the \emph{sampling} of pseudo-labels carries a prevalence
precondition that no objective can remove. Batch-statistics recalibration~\cite{adabn} is a no-op for LayerNorm. The
prior estimator is black-box shift estimation~\cite{bbse}; the proxy
$\mathcal{A}$-distance is from~\cite{bendavid}.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
Let $x$ be a waveform with label $y\in\{0,1\}$ (fake$=1$) and let $f_\theta$
produce $s_\theta(x)=P_\theta(y{=}1\mid x)$. We are given a source model, its
labelled training corpus $\mathcal{S}$, and an \emph{unlabelled} target pool
$\mathcal{T}$. No target label is read at any point.

\textbf{What breaks under corpus shift.} Ranking quality
$\mathrm{AUC}=\Pr[s_\theta(x^+){>}s_\theta(x^-)]$ is threshold-free; accuracy at
a fixed $\tau$ is not. Empirically AUC degrades far less than accuracy at
$\tau{=}0.5$ under source$\to$target shift: across $369$ third-party evaluation
cells the accuracy a model's ranking supports but its shipped threshold does not
deliver averages $7.29$ points. The deployable object is therefore the score
\emph{order}, and the broken object is the operating point. Everything below follows from taking that literally.

\textbf{(a) The target prior, label-free.} An operating point is a statement
about a prior, so we estimate the prior rather than assume it. Black-box shift
estimation~\cite{bbse} gives $\hat p = M^{-\top}q$ with
$M[y,\hat y]=P_{\mathcal S}(\hat y\mid y)$ the source confusion matrix and $q$ the
target prediction histogram. Estimators split into those reading the
\emph{thresholded prediction rate} (BBSE, EM~\cite{sld}) and those reading the
score \emph{histogram} (a two-component mixture); the first inherits whatever
calibration error the target induces, and Secs.~\ref{sec:results}
and~\ref{sec:lim} show neither is reliable where it is needed.

\textbf{(b) The tail budget, and when it is valid.} The standard rule takes the
bottom $q$ and top $q$ of the target scores as pseudo-real and pseudo-fake. Its
validity has a characterisation that needs no model at all. A bucket of $q_cN$
clips can contain at most the pool's $\pi_cN$ class-$c$ clips, so
\emph{for any detector whatsoever}
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{purity}_c\;\le\;\min\!\big(1,\ \pi_c/q_c\big).
\label{eq:purity}
\end{equation}
This is counting, not an approximation; what is empirical is its
\emph{tightness}, which is where the ranking precondition enters --- across all
seven (checkpoint, corpus) cells here the bound is never violated and is attained
to within $0.025$, the slack tracking AUC ($0.000$ at AUC $.985$, $0.025$ at
$.917$). Two consequences follow, and they are the whole story.

The \emph{symmetric} rule sets $q_c{=}q$, so perfect purity is \emph{feasible}
only while
\begin{equation}
q\;\le\;\min(\pi,\,1-\pi).
\label{eq:valid}
\end{equation}
This is \emph{necessary, not sufficient}: violating it makes pure pseudo-labels
impossible for any detector, while satisfying it only removes the prevalence
obstruction and leaves purity to the ranking. Our claims rest on the necessary
direction, which is where the published configurations fall.
In-the-Wild satisfies this at $q{=}0.3$ ($\min{=}.372$) and the recipe repairs
checkpoints there; DF does not ($\min{=}.028$), where no detector can exceed
$9.3\%$ pseudo-real purity --- we measure $8.6\%$ --- and the recipe destroys
them. The damage is one-sided: DF's pseudo-\emph{fake} tail is $100\%$ pure at
every $q$.

The \emph{prior-split} rule, $q_c{=}2q\hat\pi_c$, restores
Eq.~\eqref{eq:purity} for \emph{any} $q\le\tfrac12$ given a correct prior, and is
identical to the symmetric rule at $\hat\pi{=}0.5$. With an estimate it needs
\begin{equation}
\hat\pi_c/\pi_c\;\le\;1/(2q)\quad\text{for both classes,}
\label{eq:tol}
\end{equation}
i.e.\ at $q{=}0.3$ the estimate may overstate either class by at most $1.67\times$.
This is why it works on DF ($\hat\pi_{\mathrm{real}}/\pi_{\mathrm{real}}{=}1.03$)
and fails on In-the-Wild s42, where underestimating $\pi_{\mathrm{real}}$ inflates
the \emph{fake} budget to $0.587$ against $\pi_{\mathrm{fake}}{=}0.372$
($2.63\times$, bound $0.633$) and simply moves the impurity to the other tail.
Across the seven adapted arms we ran, the worst-tail bound is $1.000$ for the
five that came out intact ($82$--$99\%$ accuracy) and $0.093$ and $0.633$ for the
two that collapsed ($62.96$, $47.99$). Since $q$ is ours to choose and $\pi$ is
not (Sec.~\ref{sec:lim}), a third option is to shrink $q$ until
Eq.~\eqref{eq:valid} holds for the most skewed prior anticipated --- at a
supervision cost the prior-split budget avoids ($q{=}0.02$ labels $800$ of
$20{,}000$ clips against $12{,}000$). A fixed confidence threshold is a fourth,
prior-free but calibration-dependent. We report all four.

\textbf{(c) Objective.} With pseudo-labels $\tilde y$ from (b) on the confident
set $\mathcal{C}$ and a stochastic channel perturbation $a(\cdot)$ (per-clip
gain $g\sim\mathcal U(0.7,1.3)$ plus Gaussian noise $\sigma{=}0.005$),
\begin{equation}
\min_{\{\gamma,\beta\},\phi}\
\underbrace{\textstyle\sum_{x\in\mathcal C}\!\mathrm{CE}\big(f_\theta(x),\tilde y\big)}_{\mathcal L_{\mathrm{ST}}}
+\ \lambda\,\underbrace{\big\|f_\theta(a(x))-\mathrm{sg}[f_\theta(x)]\big\|^2}_{\mathcal L_{\mathrm{cons}}},
\end{equation}
optimising only the LayerNorm affine parameters $\{\gamma,\beta\}$ of the
fine-tuned blocks and the head $\phi$ ($16{,}706$ parameters), $\lambda{=}0.3$.
Restricting the trainable set and using only prior-corrected confident tails is
what prevents the degenerate collapse entropy minimisation is prone to. The
consistency term is inherited from the published recipe; an ablation finds it
contributes nothing here ($3.98$ vs $4.02$ EER), so the budget is doing the work.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\textbf{Protocol A.} The official ASVspoof2021-DF eval partition against the
official CM keys; $400{,}435$ of $611{,}829$ trials are held locally, the missing
part uniform across codec, label and phase (verified $75$--$25$ in every
stratum), true spoof rate $97.22\%$. Published pooled EERs span $1.92$~\cite{xlsrsls},
$1.95$~\cite{wav2dftsl} and $2.85$~\cite{wav2vec2aug} down to the challenge-era
best of $15.64$~\cite{asvspoof21}; our own source model reaches only $26.33$, so
we run the method on \emph{released} checkpoints instead: DeepFense
XLS-R\,300M\,+\,AASIST trained on ASVspoof2019-LA~\cite{deepfensehf}, three
independent training seeds. Adaptation uses an unlabelled $20{,}000$-clip
subsample; we report the full official pooled EER, an inductive number over
trials \emph{not} in that subsample, and two further seeds on a fixed $50{,}000$
subset, giving nine paired cells.

\textbf{Protocol B.} Leave-one-corpus-out over ASVspoof2019-LA~\cite{asvspoof19},
D2 (LibriSpeech vs.\ eight open TTS systems), In-the-Wild~\cite{itw} and the
Arabic set~\cite{arad}; each source pool merges the other three with a
38-language MLAAD subset~\cite{mlaad}, held-out target audio never appears in any
source pool, and six MLAAD languages are held out of every pool. All methods run
on the same twelve folds. \textbf{Protocol C} is all $31{,}779$ In-the-Wild clips
at their native $37.2\%$ fake rate, skewed the \emph{opposite} way to DF.

\textbf{Comparison points.} Source-only; the median-threshold rule; Tent, SHOT,
ETA/EATA, SAR; AdaBN~\cite{adabn}; DANN~\cite{dann} (which sees the held-out
target's unlabeled audio every step); an ASDG-style baseline~\cite{asdg}; and a
target-supervised oracle. Every TTA arm adapts the same $16{,}706$ parameters
with the same lr, batch and pass count; none was tuned separately, and none could
be, since per-target tuning needs the labels the setting assumes absent. Two
published constants are degenerate at $C{=}2$ and were re-derived: EATA's
redundancy filter (unreachable until the running mean hits a simplex vertex,
after which only minority-predicted samples pass) and SAR's recovery trigger
($e_0{=}0.2$ sits inside the $C{=}2$ reliable band and freezes the model; we keep
its relative position, $e_0{=}(0.2/\ln 1000)\ln C$).

\textbf{Implementation.} 16\,kHz audio, top-4-layer XLS-R fine-tuning for our own
source models (Adam, lr\,$2{\times}10^{-4}$, batch 32, 8 epochs). Adaptation:
lr\,$1{\times}10^{-4}$, $E{=}4$ passes, batch $32$ (third-party crops matched to
each checkpoint's own training length). Code and manifests:
\texttt{[URL withheld]}.

\subsection{The class prior is the hidden variable}
Table~\ref{tab:protoa} is the main result, and
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:purity}--\eqref{eq:valid} anticipate it. Applied unmodified to a
released checkpoint already better than the published Wav2Vec2-AASIST baseline
here, the symmetric-quantile configuration \emph{damages} it: accuracy
$98.87\!\to\!62.96\%$, EER $4.51\!\to\!5.84\%$, manufacturing a $31$-point deficit
where there was none.

The published baselines fail likewise, each through its own version of the
assumption. SHOT minimises $\mathbb{E}[H(p)]-H(\mathbb{E}[p])$, whose diversity
term is maximised when the mean prediction is \emph{uniform} --- at $C{=}2$ a hard
prior of $\tfrac12$; the regulariser introduced to \emph{cure} Tent-style
collapse encodes the assumption more literally than Tent does. ETA is more
instructive: its reliability filter, meant to discard the high-entropy samples
that destabilise entropy minimisation, admitted $99.8\%$ of them, so ETA reduced
to Tent and landed worse. An entropy-keyed filter separates confident from
uncertain, not \emph{confident-and-right} from \emph{confident-and-wrong}, and
mis-calibration manufactures the latter. SAR is the exception that clarifies the
rule: the only prior method that does not degrade the checkpoint, achieved by
declining to move it ($308$ recovery resets in $2{,}500$ steps). On the other two checkpoints ETA reaches $31.0$ and $37.5\%$ EER (source $3.8$,
$4.6$) and Tent $17.6$ and $11.2$. So the claim is not that every published
method fails here, but that \emph{three of five prior configurations degrade a
strong released detector at the standard benchmark's own prevalence, the fourth
avoids damage only by declining to adapt, and none improves it.}

The prior-split budget reverses this: BBSE returns $\hat\pi{=}0.9715$ against a
true $0.9708$, comfortably inside Eq.~\eqref{eq:tol}, and EER improves to
$4.02\%$. The change between the two arms is one scalar. Over three
independently trained checkpoints (EER\,\% / accuracy\,\%):

\noindent Over the nine paired cells (seed-to-seed EER std $\le0.14$),
prior-correction beats
the symmetric budget on \textbf{$9/9$} ($+1.23$ EER, Wilcoxon $p{=}0.004$) and
source-only on $8/9$ ($+0.48$, $p{=}0.027$), while the symmetric budget is worse
than source-only on $6/9$. Accuracy separates them with no overlap at all:
$98.8$--$99.1\%$ (source), $61.6$--$65.2$ (symmetric), $98.7$--$99.0$
(prior-corrected). What does \emph{not} replicate is the symmetric budget's
effect on \emph{ranking} (EER worse on two checkpoints, better on one), as
Eq.~\eqref{eq:purity} implies: a mis-specified budget corrupts the pseudo-labels
and hence the operating point, while the ranking is inherited.

\subsection{The failure, and where the recipe already works}
At In-the-Wild's native prior ($0.372$) Eq.~\eqref{eq:valid} \emph{holds}, and
the same recipe repairs rather than destroys. DeepFense s42 --- the checkpoint
that scores $4.56\%$ on DF --- arrives here ranking at AUC $0.917$ while correct
on only $39.1\%$ of clips, fewer than a majority-class predictor. Self-training
repairs exactly that: over four released checkpoints it drives the deficit from
$16.7$, $22.6$ and $44.4$ to $0.1$, $2.9$ and $5.0$ (and costs a little on the
fourth, which arrives well-calibrated at $2.1$ and has nothing to repair). On
s42 it also raises \textbf{AUC $.917\!\to\!.947$} --- which no threshold can do
--- with the disjoint half slightly better ($12.27$/$.949$), so the ranking gain
is not memorisation.

\subsection{Testing the bound by intervention}
Sweeping $q$ on DF s2 with everything else fixed gives accuracy $98.96$,
$95.68$, $88.15$, $62.96$ at $q{=}0.02,0.05,0.10,0.30$ against bounds $1.000$,
$0.556$, $0.278$, $0.093$ --- monotone in the bound (Spearman $+1.000$). At
$q{=}0.02$,
inside Eq.~\eqref{eq:valid} for this pool, the \emph{unmodified symmetric rule}
is safe and improves on source ($4.51\!\to\!4.19$) --- no prior estimate anywhere.
It remains weaker than the prior-split budget ($4.02$) for the reason
Eq.~\eqref{eq:purity} implies: shrinking $q$ fixes a minority-tail problem by
discarding the majority tail too ($800$ of $20{,}000$ clips labelled, against
$12{,}000$).

\textbf{Inducing the failure where the recipe works.} DF is one benchmark that
happens to be $97.2\%$ spoof, so we intervene on prevalence directly: resample
In-the-Wild --- where $q{=}0.3$ satisfies Eq.~\eqref{eq:valid} and repairs
checkpoints --- to a target $P(\mathrm{fake})$, holding audio and weights fixed. Accuracy at
$q{=}0.02/0.10/0.30$ is $97.8/95.9/73.3$ at $P(\mathrm{fake}){=}0.90$,
$98.6/92.6/68.5$ at $0.95$ and $98.6/89.4/65.4$ at $0.97$, against purity bounds
of $1.00/1.00/0.33$, $1.00/0.50/0.17$ and $1.00/0.30/0.10$.

\noindent Accuracy tracks the bound (Spearman $+0.966$): $q{=}0.3$ collapses
monotonically, $q{=}0.10$ degrades from $0.95$ exactly where its bound does, and
$q{=}0.02$ is unharmed. The failure belongs to prevalence, not to DF.
Necessity is what is tested: we make the condition fail, and the method fails
with it.

\textbf{Is the correction wrong, or the estimate?} On the hardest cell
(In-the-Wild s42, $\delta{=}44.4$), substituting the \emph{true} prior gives
$11.59$ EER at $91.3\%$ accuracy, against $12.58/82.4$ for the symmetric budget
and $14.51/48.0$ and $14.95/55.0$ for the BBSE and mixture estimates.

\noindent With the true prior the prior-split budget is the best method in this
paper on its hardest cell; with either estimate it is worse than using no prior.
The mechanism is sound and the estimation is not --- exactly what
Sec.~\ref{sec:lim} proves must happen.

\textbf{Not a re-thresholding result.} Both EER and AUC move, and neither takes
a threshold as input: the same prior estimate used as a threshold leaves EER at
$4.51$ by construction, and as a budget gives $4.02$.

\subsection{Cross-corpus, balanced pools: where the advantage disappears}
Where Eq.~\eqref{eq:valid} holds the prior correction is inert by construction,
so this arm measures the rest of the method. Over twelve identical folds we beat
source-only ($18.39\!\to\!17.44$ pooled EER, $p{=}0.027$), Tent ($32.25$), ETA
($29.54$), EATA ($31.34$) and SAR ($18.39$), all $p\le0.027$ --- but \textbf{not
SHOT} ($17.76$ vs.\ $17.44$, $7/12$, $p{=}0.47$), which beats us on ASVspoof2019.
That is the point: SHOT's diversity term is a hard uniform prior, free when
Eq.~\eqref{eq:valid} holds and destructive when it does not ($6.38$ EER, $77.6\%$
accuracy on DF). The separation between the two methods lives entirely in the
regime the field does not test.

\subsection{Label-free guards, and their own precondition}
Eq.~\eqref{eq:valid} says when pure pseudo-labels are feasible, but checking it
needs $\pi$, which Sec.~\ref{sec:lim} shows is not identifiable. A deployer is
left with a weaker question that \emph{can} be asked without labels --- not
\emph{will} this adaptation help but \emph{did} it --- and two statistics answer
it: rank agreement with the frozen source,
$\rho=\mathrm{Spearman}(s_\theta,s_{\theta_0})$, and the drift of the predicted
positive rate from $\hat\pi$ (Table~\ref{tab:protoa}). On DF they work
complementarily --- $\rho$ catches ranking collapse (ETA $0.31$, Tent $0.44$),
drift catches operating-point collapse ($0.37$, $0.39$, $0.23$) and is blind to
the former, and requiring both separates the five harmful arms from the three
harmless ones for any $\tau\in[0.70,0.80]$ --- and as a leave-one-target-out
\emph{stopping} rule $\rho$ recovers $13.9$ EER points from Tent's collapse
($32.25\!\to\!18.33$, oracle $17.24$) while never firing on our own method.

\textbf{But the drift monitor has a precondition of its own, and it is the same
$\delta$ the rest of the paper is about.} Repairing a mis-calibrated operating point \emph{requires} a large move in the
predicted positive rate --- that is what the repair is --- so drift separates
repair from damage only when the source already sits near the right rate. On
In-the-Wild it inverts: the two largest drifts ($0.20$, $0.50$) are the two
largest \emph{improvements} ($+17$, $+43$ accuracy points, at $\delta{=}16.7$ and
$44.4$), while the one harmful arm drifts least ($0.04$, $\delta{=}2.1$). A guard tuned on DF raises twelve false alarms there, including aborting the
best arm in this paper. The guards are sound where the source arrives well-calibrated
($\delta\!\approx\!0$, as on DF) and unusable where it does not --- the same
regime, and the same reason, as the prior estimate itself.

\subsection{Breadth, and the mechanism}
Over nine released checkpoints, five architectures and ten corpora ($369$
cells), the consistent effect is \emph{re-calibration}: $\delta$ falls
$7.29\!\to\!1.96$ ($248/369$, $p{=}2.2{\times}10^{-17}$). The mechanism is not
learned invariance --- a proxy $\mathcal{A}$-distance and a domain-origin probe
both \emph{rise} after adaptation (Arabic $1.545\!\to\!1.606$), consistent with
DANN underperforming while optimising that quantity --- and beyond any threshold
the gradient step adds separability where the ranking is strong
(within-attack-family AUC rises on all six ASVspoof2019 families, $30/30$,
$p{=}10^{-9}$).

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:lim}
\textbf{Scope.} The gain varies widely and a low-AUC start we cannot rescue.
\textbf{The prior is not identifiable, which bounds the correction.} A label-free
method sees only
$\Pr[\hat y{=}1]=\pi\,\mathrm{TPR}_{\mathcal T}+(1{-}\pi)\mathrm{FPR}_{\mathcal T}$:
one equation, three unknowns. BBSE closes it by assuming source error rates carry
over --- what covariate shift breaks --- so an extreme prior and an extreme
calibration failure look identical (DF/s2 predicts $97.4\%$ fake at a true
$0.972$, ITW/s42 $98.1\%$ at a true $0.372$). The estimate therefore degrades
exactly as the operating point does ($|\hat\pi-\pi|$ against the source deficit
gives $r{=}0.997$ over ten cells): estimating the prior and detecting that the
estimate is wrong are one problem.

\textbf{When estimation fails completely.} Our worst case is ITW/s42,
$\hat\pi{=}0.979$ against a true $0.372$ --- outside Eq.~\eqref{eq:tol} by
$2.6\times$. \emph{(i)} The prior-split budget is harmed ($14.51$ EER, $48.0\%$ accuracy, vs.\
the symmetric budget's $12.58$/$82.4$) though it still beats the unadapted source
($16.47$), so it degrades rather than collapses.
\emph{(ii)} The guards do not rescue it: the drift monitor is anchored on the
same $\hat\pi$ and inverts here, and $\rho$ exceeds $0.91$ for every In-the-Wild
arm. The safety net inherits the unidentifiability it covers.
\emph{(iii)} What survives needs no estimate, since Eq.~\eqref{eq:valid} asks
only a \emph{lower bound} on $\min(\pi,1{-}\pi)$: drop both, shrink $q$.
