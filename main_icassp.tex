Unsupervised Test-Time Adaptation for Cross-Corpus Audio Deepfake Detection}

\begin{abstract}
Audio deepfake detectors generalise poorly across corpora despite near-perfect
in-domain accuracy. We show that a fine-tuned self-supervised detector's
\emph{ranking} (ROC-AUC) largely transfers cross-corpus even when its decision
\emph{threshold} does not, and build an unsupervised \emph{test-time adaptation}
(TTA) method on that asymmetry: self-train on confident-ranked pseudo-labels
from the extreme quantiles of unlabeled target scores, enforce
channel-consistency, and adapt only LayerNorm and the classifier head---no
target labels, no source data. On a four-target, ten-seed leave-one-corpus-out
protocol, EER improves on three targets (In-the-Wild $12.78\!\to\!11.33\%$,
Arabic $22.50\!\to\!20.99\%$, ASVspoof2019 $5.03\!\to\!3.47\%$; two survive Holm
correction) and is neutral on the fourth, the one with the weakest source
ranking. Applied unmodified to nine \emph{publicly released} checkpoints we did
not train---five architectures, one a spectrogram transformer---over $257$
cross-corpus cells, it closes the gap between accuracy at the shipped threshold
and the accuracy the ranking already supports ($7.8\!\to\!1.0$ points,
$p{\approx}10^{-20}$, adapting $16{,}706$ parameters) while its effect on
ranking is not significant: the mechanism is threshold repair. Consistent with
this, a label-free rule that moves the threshold to the median score is
statistically indistinguishable from the full method over $133$ cells (both
${\sim}1$ point below a labelled oracle), and both break together when the
target pool is skewed from class balance. The EER gains that do
occur track \emph{calibration headroom}: on two corpora sharing no lineage with
the source pool (WaveFake, 2024 commercial TTS) adaptation improves EER by
$+2.5$ and $+2.9$ points ($p{=}0.002$), larger than on the original targets.
Entropy-minimisation TTA (Tent) is instead unpredictably catastrophic, and a
non-SSL backbone never reaches a ranking worth adapting.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
audio deepfake detection, anti-spoofing, test-time adaptation, cross-corpus

\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
Self-supervised (SSL) front-ends such as wav2vec\,2.0 and XLS-R, paired with
lightweight back-ends, reach very low in-domain error on standard anti-spoofing
benchmarks~\cite{wav2vec2aug,xlsrsls,aasist}. Deployed on audio from a
different source---new microphones, codecs, languages, or generators---accuracy
drops steeply, a well-documented generalisation gap~\cite{asdg,crossdomain,itw}.

We start from a diagnostic measurement: a fine-tuned XLS-R detector evaluated on
an unseen corpus keeps a high \textbf{ROC-AUC while its accuracy at a fixed
threshold collapses}---it still \emph{ranks} clips correctly but its operating
point is mis-calibrated. Because ranking transfers, its most-confident
predictions on unlabeled target audio are trustworthy enough to adapt on,
\emph{at test time}, without any labels.

Our contributions: (i) an operational use of the ranking--calibration
asymmetry---ranking transfers cross-corpus even when the threshold does
not---as the precondition that makes unsupervised self-training viable;
(ii) a simple, stable TTA method---confident-ranked
pseudo-label self-training plus channel-consistency, adapting only LayerNorm
and the head; (iii) a four-target, multilingual, ten-seed leave-one-corpus-out
evaluation against seven comparison points (source-only, Tent, BatchNorm-only
recalibration, self-training-only, a non-SSL backbone, DANN, and an
ASDG-style aggregation-separation baseline), including the
one target where the method does not help; (iv) a mechanistic analysis---proxy
$\mathcal{A}$-distance, a label-free median-threshold control that matches the
full method over $133$ cells, and a class-balance skew sweep---placing the
mechanism at \emph{operating-point repair under a balanced-prior assumption},
not domain-invariance or improved separability; and (v) evidence that the method
acts as a \emph{label-free wrapper for third-party detectors}, restoring the
operating point of nine publicly released checkpoints across five architectures
and ten corpora ($257$ cells) without retraining them.

\textbf{Related work.} Frozen or fine-tuned SSL front-ends with AASIST or
attentive back-ends dominate recent benchmarks~\cite{wav2vec2aug,xlsrsls,aasist,rawnet2};
we additionally test whether the cross-corpus gap is an SSL question at all via
a from-scratch, non-SSL backbone. Train-time domain generalisation (DG) methods align real and
separate fake across source domains (aggregation-separation~\cite{asdg}) or run
gradient-reversal domain-adversarial training over source and target jointly
(DANN)~\cite{dann}; both act at training time rather than at deployment.
Entropy minimisation (Tent)~\cite{tent} is the standard TTA recipe in vision
but, as we show, is unstable here; batch-statistics
recalibration~\cite{adabn} is a no-op for LayerNorm architectures. We borrow
the proxy $\mathcal{A}$-distance from Ben-David et al.~\cite{bendavid} to test
mechanism.

\section{Method}
Let $x$ be a waveform with label $y\in\{0,1\}$ (fake$=$1). A detector $f_\theta$
pools frame features into an embedding $e$ and outputs
$s_\theta(x)=\sigma(w^\top e+b)=P_\theta(y{=}1\mid x)$: XLS-R-300M with only its
top four transformer layers trainable, attentive temporal pooling, and a linear
head. Its normalisation is \emph{LayerNorm}, which carries no running batch
statistics, so a batch-statistics-recalibration baseline is architecturally a
no-op here and confidence-driven gradient adaptation is the minimal mechanism
that can move these layers at all.

\textbf{The ranking--calibration gap.} Ranking quality
$\mathrm{AUC}=\Pr[s_\theta(x^+){>}s_\theta(x^-)]$ is threshold-free and invariant
to reparametrisation of $s_\theta$; accuracy at a fixed threshold $\tau$ is not.
Empirically, AUC degrades far less than accuracy at $\tau{=}0.5$ under
source$\to$target shift (Fig.~\ref{fig:scores}). We therefore trust score
\emph{order}: the extreme quantiles of unlabeled target scores are
higher-precision pseudo-labels than a fixed threshold, even when $s_\theta$ is
mis-calibrated. The fixed-quantile split $Q_q$ vs.\ $Q_{1-q}$ implicitly
assumes both a reliable source ranking and roughly balanced target classes; we
scope the method to that regime, and Sec.~\ref{sec:results} and
Sec.~\ref{sec:lim} show what happens when each assumption breaks.

\textbf{Test-time adaptation.} Each epoch, we assign confident pseudo-labels
from the $q$- and $(1{-}q)$-quantiles of target scores, ignoring the middle;
self-train with cross-entropy on this confident set ($\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ST}}$);
and enforce prediction invariance under a stochastic channel perturbation
$a(\cdot)$ (gain$+$noise) via an MSE consistency term $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cons}}$
between clean and perturbed views (stop-gradient on the clean target). The
objective is
\begin{equation}
\min_{\{\gamma,\beta\},\,\phi}\ \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ST}}+\lambda\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cons}},
\qquad \lambda{=}0.3,\ q{=}0.3,\ E{=}4,
\end{equation}
optimising \emph{only} the LayerNorm affine parameters $\{\gamma,\beta\}$ of the
fine-tuned layers and the head $\phi$; the rest stays frozen. Restricting the
trainable set and using only confident tails prevents the degenerate collapse
to which entropy minimisation is prone.

\textbf{Domain divergence.} To test whether adaptation works by making source
and target indistinguishable in embedding space (as adversarial DG methods
explicitly optimise for), we compute a proxy $\mathcal{A}$-distance
$\hat d_{\mathcal{A}}=2(1{-}2\epsilon_h)$ following Ben-David et
al.~\cite{bendavid}, where $\epsilon_h$ is a linear domain classifier's
held-out error on frozen embeddings, before and after adaptation.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\textbf{Protocol.} Leave-one-corpus-out over four EER-capable targets:
ASVspoof2019-LA~\cite{asvspoof19}; D2 (LibriSpeech bona fide speech vs.\ fakes
from eight open TTS/voice-cloning systems); In-the-Wild~\cite{itw}; and the
Arabic Audio Deepfake set (ArAD)~\cite{arad}. For each held-out target the
source pool merges the other three corpora with a 38-language MLAAD
subset~\cite{mlaad} (fake-only); held-out target audio, including its
MLAAD-overlapping languages, is never in any source pool, and six MLAAD
languages are held out of every pool to test unseen-language generalisation.

\textbf{Comparison points.} Source-only; Tent~\cite{tent} (entropy minimisation,
same trainable set); BN-only (AdaBN~\cite{adabn}, no gradient step);
self-training-only ($\lambda{=}0$); Oracle (target-supervised upper bound);
RawNet2Lite (from-scratch non-SSL backbone~\cite{rawnet2}, trained identically);
DANN~\cite{dann}, trained to convergence with the held-out target's
\emph{unlabeled} inputs through a gradient-reversal branch---more privileged
access than ours; and an ASDG-style aggregation-separation
baseline~\cite{asdg}, which aligns across \emph{source} domains only.

\textbf{Implementation.} 16\,kHz audio, 3\,s crops, top-4-layer XLS-R
fine-tuning, Adam lr\,$2{\times}10^{-4}$, batch 32, source trained 8 epochs.
Adaptation: same batch and crop, lr\,$1{\times}10^{-4}$, $E{=}4$ passes;
$a(\cdot)$ is per-clip gain $g\!\sim\!\mathcal{U}(0.7,1.3)$ plus Gaussian noise
($\sigma{=}0.005$); the head carries over from the source model. Tables~\ref{tab:dann}
and \ref{tab:main} and every significance test use ten seeds per target for
every method; the third-party, $\mathcal{A}$-distance and 2021-DF studies give
their run counts inline. Code and manifests: \texttt{[URL withheld]}.

Table~\ref{tab:main} is the main result. Our method wins on three of four
targets: ASVspoof2019 ($5.03\!\to\!3.47\%$, $10/10$ seeds), Arabic
($22.50\!\to\!20.99\%$, $10/10$), In-the-Wild ($12.78\!\to\!11.33\%$, $8/10$);
on LibriSpeech-TTS it is EER-neutral ($33.54\!\to\!33.74\%$, improved in only
$5/10$ seeds), and that is also the target with the lowest source AUC ($0.73$
vs.\ $\geq\!0.84$ elsewhere). It is tempting to read source AUC as the
precondition, but the apparent $r{=}{+}0.86$ is an artefact of four clusters:
within each target the sign is reversed or absent ($r{=}{-}0.77$, ${-}0.64$,
${-}0.64$, ${+}0.12$; Fig.~\ref{fig:aucgain}), and the third-party breadth arm
below kills it outright. The precondition that does hold---calibration headroom,
and class balance---is established there.

\textbf{Significance.} Ten seeds per target make a \emph{per-target} Wilcoxon
possible at all ($n{=}10$ floor $0.002$). Against source-only, ours is
significant on ASVspoof2019 and Arabic ($p{=}0.0020$) and In-the-Wild
($p{=}0.0293$), not LibriSpeech-TTS ($p{=}0.23$); Holm correction keeps the
first two ($p_{\mathrm{adj}}{=}0.0078$) and makes In-the-Wild nominal
($0.0586$), with a bootstrap agreeing. Pooled over all 40 pairs (descriptive
only, clustered) ours beats source-only ($p{=}2.4{\times}10^{-5}$), Tent
($1.5{\times}10^{-7}$), self-training-only ($0.0056$) and both DG baselines
($30/40$ each).

\textbf{Tent is not merely unstable, it is unpredictably catastrophic.} On
ASVspoof2019 its per-seed EER splits five seeds near source-only and four at or
near chance with no warning sign (std $21.96$, mean $23.47$); on Arabic it is a
\emph{consistent} chance-level failure ($47.35{\pm}5.03\%$, $9/10$ seeds). Our
method shows no comparable collapse on any target (largest std $2.01$). The
failure is a ranking failure first: run unmodified on the strongest and weakest
public checkpoints below, Tent collapses to chance on one
($16.41\!\to\!49.80\%$ EER, AUC $.918\!\to\!.502$)~\cite{deepfensehf} but only
degrades AUC on the other ($.986\!\to\!.979$)~\cite{shiftyspeech}---an EER
collapse appears only where there was AUC headroom to lose.

\textbf{Transfer to third-party detectors.} Every result above adapts our own
source model, so we apply the published configuration unmodified to nine
released detectors we did not train---XLS-R+AASIST checkpoints trained on
WaveFake~\cite{shiftyspeech} and on ASVspoof2019~\cite{deepfensehf},
wav2vec2 sequence classifiers, and an Audio Spectrogram
Transformer~\cite{ast}---across ten corpora, $257$ cells in all.

The consistent effect is \emph{re-calibration}. Writing
$\delta{=}(100{-}\mathrm{EER}){-}\mathrm{acc}@0.5$ for the accuracy a model's
ranking supports but its shipped threshold does not deliver, adaptation drives
$\delta$ from $7.77$ to $1.04$ points ($187/257$ cells, Wilcoxon
$p{=}8.1{\times}10^{-20}$), and $\mathrm{corr}(\delta_{\text{before}},
\delta_{\text{after}}){=}{+}0.22$: where it lands barely depends on where it
started. Within families it holds wherever there is a deficit to close---the
spectrogram transformer $39/39$ ($p{=}5{\times}10^{-8}$), the WaveFake-trained
XLS-R $39/40$, one ASVspoof2019-trained XLS-R $38/40$ ($p{<}10^{-11}$)---is a
no-op on families whose shipped threshold is already near-optimal, and
\emph{worsens} calibration on one wav2vec2 classifier ($2/12$). That the same
LayerNorm-and-head selection rule, designed against XLS-R, re-calibrates a
mel-spectrogram transformer unchanged is the clearest architecture-independence
evidence here.

Ranking is a different matter. Pooled over the same cells the EER change is
\emph{not} significant ($+0.46\%$, $p{=}0.16$; AUC $+0.005$, $p{=}0.04$).
Per cell at ten seeds with Holm correction, $8/12$ tested cells are
significant---but four of those are significant \emph{degradations}, the largest
$38.13\!\to\!41.91\%$. Our earlier single-corpus result (In-the-Wild, four
checkpoints, all improved, $16.41\!\to\!10.10\%$ on the weakest) reproduces but
does not generalise. It is not memorisation: adapting on half of In-the-Wild and
scoring the disjoint half gives $4.08\%$ vs.\ $4.06\%$ transductive.

\textbf{The method is a label-free threshold rule.} If the entire benefit is a
shifted operating point, gradient descent should not be needed to obtain it. For
each source checkpoint we score the pool once and threshold at the \emph{median}
score (label-free; the split predicting $50\%$ positive). Over $70$ of our own
cells the median rule reaches $75.3\%$ accuracy vs.\ TTA's $75.6\%$ (labelled
oracle $76.6\%$)---$94\%$ of TTA's gain with no training and none of the
$16{,}706$ adapted parameters. Over $63$ third-party cells it \emph{beats} TTA,
$75.6$ vs.\ $73.7\%$ ($p{=}0.04$, within $0.4$ of the oracle): where adaptation
degrades a checkpoint's ranking a threshold move cannot, so it is the safer of
the two. Pooled over all $133$ cells the two are statistically indistinguishable,
both ${\sim}1$ point under the labelled oracle. Both assume a class-balanced pool
and break together when that fails (Sec.~\ref{sec:lim}). The gradient method is
doing, expensively, what a one-line threshold move does at least as well.

\textbf{Where EER gains do occur, they track calibration headroom, and lineage
predicts headroom.} Extending our own model past the four original targets:
three ASVspoof2021 tracks (LA/DF/PA) erase the pooled EER benefit
($+0.88\!\to\!{-}0.68$, $n{=}70$, $p{=}0.80$)---but these are relatives of
ASVspoof2019, which is \emph{in} the source pool, so they arrive already
well-thresholded (deficit ${-}2.7$ to ${+}3.2$) with nothing to repair. Two
corpora that share no lineage with any source---WaveFake (vocoder artefacts over
LJSpeech)~\cite{shiftyspeech} and a 2024 commercial-TTS set (ElevenLabs, Polly,
Kokoro, Hume, Speechify)---instead arrive badly mis-thresholded (deficit $15.6$,
$5.8$) and adaptation improves their EER by $+2.5$ and $+2.9$ points ($10$ seeds
each, $p{=}0.002$), \emph{larger} than any original target. Pooling the six
genuinely independent targets gives $+1.48$ EER ($n{=}60$, $p{<}10^{-8}$). The
positive EER numbers are contingent not on which targets were to hand but on
calibration headroom, which lineage-relatedness predicts.

\textbf{The adaptation budget is a confound we introduced.} $E$ counts epochs,
so the number of updates scales with pool size: $E{=}4$ over In-the-Wild's
$31{,}779$ clips is $5.3\times$ the steps of $E{=}4$ over a $6{,}000$-clip
pool. On our own model at ten seeds, $E{=}32$ lifts the Arabic gain from $+1.41$ to
$+3.65$ EER on $10/10$ seeds ($p{=}0.002$), while on LibriSpeech-TTS---the
weakest source ranking---it does not help ($2/10$). Handed the same $8\times$
budget the baselines do not benefit (Tent worsens by $5$ EER points on Arabic,
self-training-only is flat), so the gain is the method's, not the compute's.
On third-party checkpoints, both significant AST \emph{degradations} reverse at
$E{=}32$: EER gain $-3.78\!\to\!{+}7.24$ and $-2.14\!\to\!{+}1.86$, positive on
$6/6$ seeds each ($p{=}0.031$). $E$ belongs in step counts, not epochs; but more
steps is not a free win (it hurts the weakest-ranking target monotonically), so
we report the defect rather than re-tabulate around it.

\textbf{Backbone and prior DG baselines.} RawNet2Lite (no SSL pretraining)
stays near chance on every target (AUC $0.40$--$0.65$) even retrained for 40
epochs with stronger regularisation, so cross-corpus viability starts from SSL
pretraining, not the TTA step. Both train-time DG baselines are beaten pooled
over all 40 pairs ($30/40$ each); per target we beat DANN on two of four
($p{\le}0.0098$; tie on In-the-Wild and LibriSpeech-TTS) and ASDG on three of
four, with ASDG better on LibriSpeech-TTS ($32.64$ vs.\ $33.74\%$). DANN sees
the held-out target's unlabeled audio every training step yet is worse, costs
$3\times$ as much, and is seed-unstable (ASVspoof2019 EER $3.23$--$14.85\%$);
ASDG beats plain source-only on only two of four targets (Table~\ref{tab:dann}).
Neither is a reliable route to cross-corpus robustness here.

\textbf{Mechanism.} An inductive check (adapt and evaluate on disjoint target
halves) rules out transductive memorisation. The proxy $\mathcal{A}$-distance
and a linear domain-origin probe both \emph{increase} slightly after adaptation
(Arabic $\hat d_{\mathcal{A}}\!:1.545\!\to\!1.606$), the opposite of what
domain-invariance predicts: adaptation re-shapes the boundary with
rank-reliable pseudo-labels rather than erasing domain identity, consistent with
DANN underperforming despite optimising that quantity. MLAAD fake-clip recall is
uniform across seen and unseen languages ($93.9$ vs.\ $93.3\%$).

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:lim}
The method is transductive (unlabeled target inputs at adaptation time, the
standard TTA setting); an inductive check reproduces every result including the
LibriSpeech-TTS null. We report per-target, per-seed numbers throughout, so the
limitations below can be read against the evidence:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Needs an already-reliable source ranking---and we cannot say how
much of that is SSL.} A from-scratch or low-AUC starting point is not a regime
we can rescue (Fig.~\ref{fig:aucgain}). Our non-SSL backbone never reaches a
ranking worth adapting (AUC $0.40$--$0.65$), so it fails the precondition
before TTA becomes applicable and we did not run adaptation on it; every
third-party checkpoint we adapt is itself a pretrained transformer.
\item \textbf{Class balance is a second precondition, and it is the one the
operator can check without labels.} The fixed-quantile rule labels the bottom
$q$ of the pool ``confident real'' whatever the true prior, so it targets a
\emph{balanced}-prior operating point. A controlled skew sweep (our model, four
targets) confirms the mechanism: as the pool goes $50\!\to\!70\!\to\!90\%$ fake,
the accuracy gain goes $+7.6\!\to\!{-}3.5\!\to\!{-}26.5$ points while ranking
stays flat---at $90\%$ fake, adaptation drags a $87.3\%$ source model to
$60.8\%$. This is the same failure seen once on the official ASVspoof2021-DF
protocol ($97.2\%$-spoof pool: source $26.33\%$ EER, TTA $42.46\%$), and
label-free corrections do not fix it (prevalence-weighted budget: $32.58\%$ /
$42.46\%$ under two source recipes; $q$-curriculum: $42.50\%$). Deployment
prevalence is normally known, so we state it as a precondition rather than
estimate it from audio. (Our source model is also not state-of-the-art---$8$--$9\%$
EER is reported on 2021-DF by SSL+AASIST systems against our $26.33\%$
source-only---so this is a \emph{relative}-gain claim on a fixed backbone, to be
re-verified on a stronger one.)
\item \textbf{Fixed hyperparameters.} $q$ and $\lambda$ are label-free and not
per-target optimal; a label-free $\lambda$ schedule pays nothing at our scale
(within $0.24$ EER of a per-point oracle). $E$ is not a hyperparameter but a
mis-specified budget, as above.
\item \textbf{Scale.} The main table is four targets, ten seeds; going from
three to ten seeds made the per-target tests possible but shrank our DG margin
to two per-target wins against DANN and three against ASDG. Breadth comes from
the third-party arm (nine detectors, ten corpora, $257$ cells) and six
additional ten-seed targets for our own model, which is where the source-AUC
ordering was falsified and the calibration account confirmed. The mechanistic
claims (threshold rule, balanced-prior assumption) are established on our model
and the label-free control; the two large HF checkpoints run at batch $8$ not
$16$, and three of the nine third-party checkpoints have undocumented training
data and are kept in a separate arm.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
Cross-corpus audio deepfake detection fails because the operating point is
mis-calibrated, not because ranking is lost. Our unsupervised TTA repairs the
operating point---closing the shipped-vs-attainable accuracy gap on nine
third-party detectors over $257$ cells ($p{\approx}10^{-20}$) while leaving
ranking unchanged---and a label-free move of the threshold to the median score
matches it over $133$ cells with no training; both require a class-balanced
target pool. The EER gains that remain are conditional on calibration headroom, which
lineage-relatedness predicts (independent WaveFake and commercial-TTS targets
gain $+2.5$/$+2.9$ EER). Source AUC does not predict the gain and is retired.
It is a re-calibration wrapper, not a detector.
