\begin{abstract}
Audio deepfake detectors generalise poorly across corpora despite near-perfect
in-domain accuracy. We show the failure is one of \emph{operating point}: a
fine-tuned self-supervised detector's \emph{ranking} (ROC-AUC) largely transfers
cross-corpus while its decision \emph{threshold} does not, so unlabeled target
audio can be used to repair the operating point at test time. We further show
that every confident-quantile test-time adaptation (TTA) recipe silently assumes
a \emph{class-balanced} target pool---an assumption every TTA benchmark in this
area satisfies and no deployment does. On the official ASVspoof2021-DF eval
partition ($400{,}435$ trials, $97.2\%$ spoof) the published symmetric-quantile
configuration \emph{damages} a state-of-the-art released checkpoint, driving
accuracy $98.87\!\to\!62.96\%$ and EER $4.51\!\to\!5.84\%$. Correcting the
pseudo-label budget with a label-free estimate of the target prior obtained by
black-box shift estimation ($\hat\pi{=}0.9715$ vs.\ a true $0.9708$) instead
improves the same checkpoint to $4.02\%$ EER---an $11\%$ relative reduction,
with no target labels, no retraining, and $16{,}706$ adapted parameters. Because
both EER and AUC improve, no re-thresholding rule can account for the gain; the
one-line median-threshold control that appears competitive on balanced pools
loses $40$ accuracy points at this benchmark's real prior. We add a label-free
stopping rule based on rank agreement with the source model, and compare against
Tent, SHOT, ETA/EATA and SAR at both balanced and deployment prevalence.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
audio deepfake detection, anti-spoofing, test-time adaptation, label shift,
cross-corpus generalisation, EER.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
Self-supervised (SSL) front-ends such as wav2vec\,2.0 and XLS-R, paired with
lightweight back-ends, reach very low in-domain error on standard anti-spoofing
benchmarks~\cite{wav2vec2aug,xlsrsls,aasist}. Deployed on audio from a
different source---new microphones, codecs, languages, or generators---accuracy
drops steeply, a well-documented generalisation gap~\cite{asdg,crossdomain,itw}.

We start from a diagnostic measurement: a fine-tuned XLS-R detector evaluated on
an unseen corpus keeps a high \textbf{ROC-AUC while its accuracy at a fixed
threshold collapses}. It still \emph{ranks} clips correctly; its operating point
is mis-calibrated. Because ranking transfers, the most-confident predictions on
unlabeled target audio are trustworthy enough to adapt on at test time.

That observation is the starting point of a family of methods, not a method.
The step every member of the family takes---label the extreme quantiles of the
target scores and self-train on them---carries an assumption that is invisible
on the benchmarks the field uses and fatal on the pools it deploys to: that the
target pool is roughly class-balanced. Take the top and bottom $30\%$ of a
$97\%$-spoof pool and the ``confidently bona fide'' bucket is mechanically made
of low-scoring \emph{spoof}. Self-training then teaches the detector that a
great deal of spoof is genuine. This is not hypothetical: it is what happens to
a released state-of-the-art checkpoint on the official ASVspoof2021-DF
protocol (Table~\ref{tab:protoa}).

\textbf{Contributions.} (i) The ranking--calibration asymmetry as the mechanism
of cross-corpus failure, established over $369$ third-party evaluation cells and
tested directly---a proxy $\mathcal{A}$-distance \emph{rises} after adaptation,
so the effect is operating-point repair, not learned invariance.
(ii) The class-prior assumption hidden in confident-quantile TTA, measured on
the field's standard benchmark at its real prevalence, where it inverts the sign
of the published method's effect on a SOTA checkpoint.
(iii) A label-free correction: black-box shift estimation of the target prior,
used to split the pseudo-label budget, which turns that damage into an $11\%$
relative EER reduction on the official protocol---threshold-free, so no
re-thresholding rule explains it.
(iv) A label-free budget: rank agreement with the frozen source model both
selects the number of adaptation steps and predicts entropy-minimisation
collapse before it happens.

\textbf{Related work.} Frozen or fine-tuned SSL front-ends with AASIST or
attentive back-ends dominate recent benchmarks~\cite{wav2vec2aug,xlsrsls,aasist,rawnet2}.
Train-time domain generalisation aligns real and separates fake across source
domains (aggregation--separation~\cite{asdg}) or runs gradient-reversal
domain-adversarial training over source and target jointly (DANN)~\cite{dann};
both act at training time rather than at deployment. In TTA, entropy
minimisation (Tent)~\cite{tent} is the reference recipe; its instability
prompted reliable-sample filtering and anti-forgetting (ETA/EATA)~\cite{eata},
sharpness-aware entropy with a recovery scheme (SAR)~\cite{sar}, and
information maximisation with self-labelling (SHOT)~\cite{shot}. We compare
against all four. Batch-statistics recalibration~\cite{adabn} is a no-op for
LayerNorm architectures. Our prior estimator is black-box shift
estimation~\cite{bbse}; the proxy $\mathcal{A}$-distance is from Ben-David et
al.~\cite{bendavid}.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
Let $x$ be a waveform with label $y\in\{0,1\}$ (fake$=1$) and let $f_\theta$
produce $s_\theta(x)=P_\theta(y{=}1\mid x)$. We are given a source model, its
labelled training corpus $\mathcal{S}$, and an \emph{unlabelled} target pool
$\mathcal{T}$. No target label is read at any point.

\textbf{What breaks under corpus shift.} Ranking quality
$\mathrm{AUC}=\Pr[s_\theta(x^+){>}s_\theta(x^-)]$ is threshold-free; accuracy at
a fixed $\tau$ is not. Empirically AUC degrades far less than accuracy at
$\tau{=}0.5$ under source$\to$target shift (Fig.~\ref{fig:scores}). The
deployable object is therefore the score \emph{order}, and the broken object is
the operating point. Everything below follows from taking that literally.

\textbf{(a) The target prior, label-free.} An operating point is a statement
about a prior, so we estimate the prior rather than assume it. Black-box shift
estimation~\cite{bbse} gives $\hat p = M^{-\top}q$, where
$M[y,\hat y]=P_{\mathcal S}(\hat y\mid y)$ is the source confusion matrix
measured on the model's own labelled training data and $q$ is the observed
target prediction histogram. $M$ and $q$ read source error structure and target
\emph{prediction rate} only---never the shape of the target score distribution,
which is precisely what mis-calibration corrupts. That is why BBSE survives here
where score-histogram estimators (a two-component GMM, Otsu, $k$-means) do not:
on a controlled skew sweep BBSE tracks the prior to within $1.4\%$ where the GMM
is $28\%$ off at $90\%$ fake.

\textbf{(b) Prior-corrected confident tails.} The standard rule takes the bottom
$q$ and top $q$ of the target scores as pseudo-real and pseudo-fake. We split the
same total budget $2q$ by the estimated prior,
\begin{equation}
q_{\mathrm{real}} = 2q\,\hat\pi_{\mathrm{real}},\qquad
q_{\mathrm{fake}} = 2q\,(1-\hat\pi_{\mathrm{real}}),
\label{eq:budget}
\end{equation}
which is \emph{identical} to the symmetric rule at $\hat\pi_{\mathrm{real}}=0.5$
--- so on a balanced pool the correction is provably inert and cannot be the
source of any balanced-pool result --- and shrinks the pseudo-real bucket to the
estimated real fraction otherwise. The prior is estimated \emph{once}, from the
frozen pre-adaptation model: re-estimating each epoch lets a drifting model
reinforce its own mistakes.

\textbf{(c) Objective.} With pseudo-labels $\tilde y$ from (b) on the confident
set $\mathcal{C}$ and a stochastic channel perturbation $a(\cdot)$ (per-clip
gain $g\sim\mathcal U(0.7,1.3)$ plus Gaussian noise $\sigma{=}0.005$),
\begin{equation}
\min_{\{\gamma,\beta\},\phi}\
\underbrace{\textstyle\sum_{x\in\mathcal C}\!\mathrm{CE}\big(f_\theta(x),\tilde y\big)}_{\mathcal L_{\mathrm{ST}}}
+\ \lambda\,\underbrace{\big\|f_\theta(a(x))-\mathrm{sg}[f_\theta(x)]\big\|^2}_{\mathcal L_{\mathrm{cons}}},
\end{equation}
optimising only the LayerNorm affine parameters $\{\gamma,\beta\}$ of the
fine-tuned blocks and the head $\phi$ ($16{,}706$ parameters), with
$\lambda{=}0.3$, $q{=}0.3$. Restricting the trainable set and using only
prior-corrected confident tails is what prevents the degenerate collapse to
which entropy minimisation is prone.

\textbf{(d) A label-free budget, and a collapse guard.} Adaptation needs a stop,
and the number of steps has been the weakest-specified part of TTA recipes in
this area. Our thesis says the source \emph{ranking} is the asset being
exploited, so an update that destroys it has discarded its own precondition ---
and a TTA collapse \emph{is} a ranking collapse. We monitor
$\rho_t=\mathrm{Spearman}\big(s_{\theta_t}(\mathcal T),\,s_{\theta_0}(\mathcal T)\big)$,
computable without labels, and keep the last step with $\rho_t\ge\tau$. A second
monitor, $|\Pr[s_{\theta_t}{>}\tfrac12]-\hat\pi_{\mathrm{fake}}|$, flags a model
that is re-labelling the pool rather than re-calibrating on it.
Sec.~\ref{sec:results} validates both leave-one-target-out against the oracle
budget, with churn and confidence as controls.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\textbf{Protocol A (published baselines exist).} The official ASVspoof2021-DF
eval partition, scored against the official CM keys. We hold $400{,}435$ of the
$611{,}829$ trials locally; the missing part is uniform across codec, label and
phase (verified $75$--$25$ in every stratum), so the pooled EER is an unbiased
estimate of the full-set number, and we report $n$ throughout. True spoof rate
$97.22\%$. Because our own source model is not competitive on this protocol
($26.33\%$ EER against a published $8.54\%$), we run the method on \emph{released
third-party checkpoints} instead: DeepFense's XLS-R\,300M\,+\,AASIST detectors
trained on ASVspoof2019-LA train~\cite{deepfensehf}, at three independent
training seeds. Adaptation uses an unlabelled $20{,}000$-clip subsample of the
eval pool; we report the full official pooled EER and, as an inductive check,
the EER restricted to trials \emph{not} in that subsample.

\textbf{Protocol B (cross-corpus, balanced).} Leave-one-corpus-out over four
EER-capable targets: ASVspoof2019-LA~\cite{asvspoof19}; D2 (LibriSpeech bona
fide vs.\ eight open TTS/voice-cloning systems); In-the-Wild~\cite{itw}; and the
Arabic Audio Deepfake set~\cite{arad}. For each held-out target the source pool
merges the other three with a 38-language MLAAD subset~\cite{mlaad}
(fake-only); held-out target audio is never in any source pool and six MLAAD
languages are held out of every pool. Ten seeds per target per method.

\textbf{Protocol C (a benchmark at its own prevalence).} All $31{,}779$
In-the-Wild clips --- the whole benchmark, not the $6{,}000$-clip subsample the
third-party arm uses --- at its native $37.2\%$ fake rate, which is skewed the
\emph{opposite} way to DF's $97.2\%$.

\textbf{Comparison points.} Source-only; the median-threshold rule (label-free,
no gradient); Tent~\cite{tent}; SHOT~\cite{shot}; ETA and EATA~\cite{eata};
SAR~\cite{sar}; AdaBN~\cite{adabn}; DANN~\cite{dann} (trained to convergence
with the held-out target's unlabeled inputs --- more privileged access than
ours); an ASDG-style aggregation--separation baseline~\cite{asdg}; and a
target-supervised oracle. Two published constants in the modern TTA baselines
are vacuous at $C{=}2$ and were re-derived rather than transplanted: EATA's
redundancy threshold ($|\cos|{<}0.05$ is unreachable on the binary simplex,
whose minimum attainable cosine is ${\approx}0.12$, so we run the reliability
filter alone --- EATA's own published ablation) and SAR's recovery trigger
($e_0{=}0.2$ sits \emph{inside} the $C{=}2$ reliable band $H{<}0.277$ and
freezes the model; we keep its relative position, $e_0=(0.2/\ln 1000)\ln C$).

\textbf{Implementation.} 16\,kHz audio, top-4-layer XLS-R fine-tuning for our own
source models (Adam, lr\,$2{\times}10^{-4}$, batch 32, 8 epochs). Adaptation:
lr\,$1{\times}10^{-4}$, $E{=}4$ passes, batch $32$ (third-party crops matched to
each checkpoint's own training length). Code and manifests:
\texttt{[URL withheld]}.

\subsection{The class prior is the hidden variable}
Table~\ref{tab:protoa} is the main result. Applied unmodified to a released
checkpoint that is already better than the published Wav2Vec2-AASIST baseline
on this protocol, the symmetric-quantile configuration \emph{damages} it:
accuracy $98.87\!\to\!62.96\%$, EER $4.51\!\to\!5.84\%$, and it manufactures a
$31$-point calibration deficit where there was none. The mechanism is
mechanical: $q{=}0.3$ labels the bottom $30\%$ of a $97.2\%$-spoof pool
``confidently bona fide''.

Replacing the symmetric budget by Eq.~\eqref{eq:budget} with a label-free prior
estimate reverses the sign. BBSE returns $\hat\pi_{\mathrm{fake}}{=}0.9715$
against a true $0.9708$, giving a budget of $(0.017,0.583)$ instead of
$(0.3,0.3)$, and EER improves to $4.02\%$ --- an $11\%$ relative reduction on
the official protocol with no target labels and no retraining. The change
between the two arms is one scalar. Across three independently trained
checkpoints and \PENDING{stage 2/5: seeds} seeds the effect is
\PENDING{stage 2/5: paired test}.

\subsection{Why this is not a re-thresholding result}
Both EER and AUC improve, and neither takes a threshold as input, so no
operating-point rule can produce this gain. That matters because on
\emph{balanced} pools a one-line median-threshold rule is genuinely competitive
with gradient adaptation on accuracy --- we reported it as such, and it is the
right control for a calibration claim. Resampling the same DF score
distribution to a range of target priors shows why it was competitive and what
it costs:

\textbf{Tent is not merely unstable, it is unpredictably catastrophic.} On
ASVspoof2019 its per-seed EER splits five seeds near source-only and four at or

\emph{consistent} chance-level failure ($9/10$ seeds). Ours shows no comparable

first: run on the strongest and weakest public checkpoints, Tent collapses to
chance on one ($16.41\!\to\!49.80\%$ EER, AUC $.918\!\to\!.502$) but only
degrades AUC on the other ($.986\!\to\!.979$) --- an EER collapse appears only
where there was AUC headroom to lose.

\subsection{Breadth, and the mechanism}
Applied unmodified to nine released checkpoints across five architectures and
ten corpora ($369$ cells), the consistent effect is \emph{re-calibration}:
writing $\delta{=}(100{-}\mathrm{EER}){-}\mathrm{acc}@0.5$, adaptation drives
$\delta$ from $7.29$ to $1.96$ points ($248/369$ cells, Wilcoxon
$p{=}2.2{\times}10^{-17}$), with
$\mathrm{corr}(\delta_{\text{before}},\delta_{\text{after}}){=}{+}0.17$: where it
lands barely depends on where it started. That the same LayerNorm-and-head
selection rule, designed against XLS-R, re-calibrates a mel-spectrogram
transformer~\cite{ast} unchanged ($39/39$ cells, $p{=}5{\times}10^{-8}$) is the
clearest architecture-independence evidence here.

The mechanism is \emph{not} learned invariance. A proxy $\mathcal{A}$-distance
and a linear domain-origin probe both \emph{increase} after adaptation (Arabic
$\hat d_{\mathcal{A}}\!:1.545\!\to\!1.606$), the opposite of what
domain-invariance predicts --- consistent with DANN underperforming despite
optimising exactly that quantity. What the gradient step does add, beyond any
threshold, is separability where the source ranking is strong: on ASVspoof2019
it raises within-attack-family AUC on all six attack families ($30/30$
attack$\times$seed cells, $p{=}10^{-9}$), largest on the two hardest; on
AUC-$0.71$ LibriSpeech-TTS there is nothing to sharpen and the effect is null.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:lim}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{BBSE assumes label shift; real corpora also shift covariates.}
Its prior estimate carries a positive bias that is largest at balanced priors
($\hat\pi{=}0.61$ at a true $0.50$ on the DF distribution) and shrinks toward the
truth at high skew ($0.9738$ at $0.9722$), because the in-domain confusion
matrix under-states target error under codec shift. The correction is therefore
most accurate exactly where it matters most, but it is not a general prior
estimator.
\item \textbf{The prior fixes the budget, not the threshold.} On a checkpoint
whose shipped threshold is already near-optimal, the BBSE \emph{threshold} buys
nothing over $\tau{=}0.5$ ($98.94$ vs.\ $98.92$). The gain reported here comes
from the pseudo-label budget.
\item \textbf{Needs an already-reliable source ranking.} A low-AUC starting
point is not a regime we can rescue: a from-scratch non-SSL backbone never
reaches a ranking worth adapting (AUC $0.40$--$0.65$), and the AUC-$0.71$
target is the one null in Table~\ref{tab:main}. Every third-party checkpoint we
adapt is itself a pretrained transformer.
\item \textbf{Relative, not absolute, on our own model.} We improve released
checkpoints; we did not train a Protocol-A-competitive detector ourselves
($26.33\%$ vs.\ a published $8.54\%$), so all claims here are of the form
``this wrapper improves that detector''.
\item \textbf{Fixed hyper-parameters.} $q$ and $\lambda$ are label-free and not
per-target optimal; a label-free $\lambda$ schedule pays nothing at our scale
(within $0.24$ EER of a per-point oracle).
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
Cross-corpus audio deepfake detection fails at the operating point, not in the
representation, and the repair can be done at test time without labels. But the
recipe the field uses to do it assumes a class-balanced target pool --- an
assumption its benchmarks satisfy and its deployments do not. On the official
ASVspoof2021-DF protocol at its real $97.2\%$ spoof prior, that assumption turns
the published configuration into a $1.3$-point EER \emph{regression} on a
state-of-the-art released checkpoint; a label-free estimate of the prior turns
it into an $11\%$ relative improvement. Because both EER and AUC move, the
result is not re-thresholding; and because the correction is provably inert at
$\hat\pi{=}0.5$, it costs nothing on the balanced benchmarks where the field
currently measures.
