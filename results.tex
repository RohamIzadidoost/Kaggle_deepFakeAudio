\section*{Main results}
Each column is a held-out target. each row is a different method
(Source-only = no adaptation at all. BN-only = no-op control since our encoder has
no BatchNorm stats. Tent = a known TTA baseline that collapses on 2 targets.

supervised upper bound). Ours wins on 3 targets out of 4; each cell is
EER\%\,/\,AUC, lower/higher is better.

\section*{Gain decomposition}
Splits the total EER change into two steps: $\Delta_1$ = effect of adding
self-training alone, $\Delta_2$ = effect of then adding consistency on top.
$\Delta_2$ is negative (helps) on 3 targets but +0.59 on LibriSpeech-TTS
\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{@{}lccc@{}}
\toprule
Target & Source EER & $\Delta_1$ (self-train) & $\Delta_2$ (consistency) \\
\midrule
ASVspoof2019 & 4.76 & $-0.69$ & $-0.39$ \\
LibriSpeech-TTS & 34.36 & $-0.57$ & \cellcolor{red!10}$+0.59$ \\
In-the-Wild & 12.57 & $-0.94$ & $-0.79$ \\
Arabic & 22.59 & $-0.54$ & $-1.31$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\newpage
\section*{Comparison to train-time domain-adversarial training (DANN)}
DANN sees the target's unlabeled audio during training (more access than we
get) yet is worse than Source-only on 2 targets and barely better on the
others. Ours beats DANN on every target at $\sim\!1/3$ the compute
(9 vs.\ 30 min per target-seed).
\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{@{}lccc@{}}
\toprule
Target & Source & DANN (30 min) & Ours (9 min) \\
\midrule
ASVspoof2019 & 4.76 / .990 & 6.61 / .980 & \textbf{3.69} / .994 \\
LibriSpeech-TTS & 34.36 / .720 & 34.81 / .724 & \textbf{34.38} / .722 \\
In-the-Wild & 12.57 / .944 & 11.95 / .931 & \textbf{10.83} / .960 \\
Arabic & 22.59 / .854 & 29.51 / .724 & \textbf{20.74} / .873 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\section*{Inductive check}
Rules out ``the model just memorised the scored clips'': adapt on one half of
the target, score on the disjoint other half. The main table's pattern
replicates almost exactly (wins on 3 out of 4,), so the gain
is real, not memorisation. 
\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcc@{}}
\toprule
Target & Source (half) & Ours (disjoint half) \\
\midrule
ASVspoof2019 & 4.93 & \textbf{4.16} \\
LibriSpeech-TTS & 34.49 & 34.70 \\
In-the-Wild & 12.66 & \textbf{11.13} \\
Arabic & 22.29 & \textbf{21.14} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\newpage
\section*{Domain divergence before/after adaptation}
$\hat d_\mathcal{A}$ and domain-probe
accuracy both measure how easily a linear classifier can tell source from
target embeddings apart; both go \emph{up} slightly after adaptation on every
target, showing adaptation works by re-shaping the decision boundary, not by
erasing domain identity (which is what DANN tries to do, and underperforms
at) and i tries DANN but failed.
\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcccc@{}}
\toprule
& \multicolumn{2}{c}{$\hat d_{\mathcal{A}}$} & \multicolumn{2}{c}{Domain probe acc.\ (\%)} \\
Target & source & adapted & source & adapted \\
\midrule
ASVspoof2019 & 1.572 & 1.609 & 86.6 & 89.9 \\
LibriSpeech-TTS & 1.460 & 1.504 & 86.4 & 86.6 \\
In-the-Wild & 1.335 & 1.357 & 80.4 & 81.6 \\
Arabic & 1.545 & 1.606 & 87.9 & 89.1 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}
